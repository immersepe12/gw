Counseling

Counseling and Therapy in Modesto

Generations Wellness offers outpatient counseling for adults with licensed counselors, in person at our downtown Modesto office or by secure telehealth anywhere in California. Counseling is talk therapy for anxiety, depression, grief, stress and the strain of living with illness or pain, inside a physician-led practice.

Who We Help

What Counseling Can Help With

Counseling, also called psychotherapy or talk therapy, is a structured conversation with a trained, licensed professional. You talk through what is hard, notice patterns in your thoughts and habits, and practice new ways of coping. The National Institute of Mental Health describes psychotherapy as a way to help people recognize and change troubling emotions, thoughts and behaviors. It is used on its own and alongside medical care.

At launch, our counseling service is for adults 18 and older. People often come to us for:

  • Anxiety and worry that will not switch off, panic attacks, or stress that gets in the way of work and sleep. Read more about counseling for anxiety.
  • Depression and low mood, including feeling flat, losing interest in things you used to enjoy, or depression after having a baby. See counseling for depression.
  • Grief and big life changes such as the death of someone close, divorce, retirement, a move or a new diagnosis. See grief and life transitions counseling.
  • Living with chronic illness or pain, where the condition affects your mood, sleep, relationships and work. See counseling for chronic illness and pain.
  • Everyday stress and burnout from caregiving, family pressure or work.

Parents asking about help for a teen or child can read counseling for teens and children. Services for younger clients depend on clinician availability, so please call and ask.

You do not need a diagnosis to start. Many people come in simply because something feels heavier than it should, and they want a place to sort it out.

How It Works

Starting Counseling, Step by Step

  1. Call us. Phone either number below and tell us you are interested in counseling. You do not need a referral.
  2. A short screening call. A brief, private phone conversation covers what you would like help with, whether you prefer in-person or telehealth sessions, and a few safety questions. This helps us make sure our outpatient service is the right fit. If you need a different kind of care, we will tell you honestly and point you toward it.
  3. Paperwork before you start. You receive intake forms, a consent form that explains confidentiality and its legal limits, a telehealth consent if you choose online sessions, and two short questionnaires about mood and anxiety. Self-pay clients also receive a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges.
  4. Your first session. The first appointment is an assessment. Your counselor asks about what brings you in, your history, your health and your goals. Together you agree on a plan: what you want to work on and how often to meet.
  5. Ongoing sessions. Most people meet weekly or every other week. Sessions are usually about 50 minutes. Your counselor checks in on progress along the way, and you can change the plan at any time.

What happens in a session

Counselors use approaches with research behind them, chosen to fit you. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), for example, looks at how thoughts, feelings and actions affect each other and builds practical skills. Other approaches focus on acceptance, problem-solving, or understanding relationships. You will not be asked to share more than you are ready to share.

Your Choice

In-Person or Telehealth Sessions

You can meet your counselor in a private room at our office at 1801 H Street in downtown Modesto, or by video from home. Many clients from Ceres, Turlock, Riverbank, Salida and Oakdale find telehealth easier on busy weeks, and some mix the two.

  • Telehealth uses a secure, health-privacy-compliant video platform, not a regular consumer video app.
  • You need to be physically in California during each telehealth session, because California licenses cover clients located in the state.
  • At each telehealth visit, your counselor confirms where you are and an emergency contact, so help can be sent if it is ever needed.
  • Choose a private spot. A parked car, a closed bedroom or headphones on a walk can all work.

Connected Care

Counseling Inside a Physician-Led Practice

Most counseling offices stand alone. Ours sits inside a medical practice that also offers pain management, physical therapy, wound care and other outpatient services under the direction of Dr. Rajwinder Singh Bahia, MD. That matters because the body and mind affect each other. Long healing, ongoing pain and serious diagnoses can wear down anyone's mood, and stress can make pain and sleep worse.

With your written permission, your counselor can share a short summary with your physician here, such as your goals and any safety concerns, so your care fits together. Your detailed therapy notes stay with your counselor. You decide what is shared, and you can change your mind.

What about medication?

Counseling at our practice is talk therapy. Counselors do not prescribe. If you have questions about medicine for mood or anxiety, talk with a physician, such as your primary care doctor. Some people use counseling alone, and some use it alongside medicine their doctor manages.

Paying for Counseling

Cost, Good Faith Estimates and Superbills

Counseling is offered on a self-pay basis. Call us to ask about current pricing and payment options; we review costs with you before treatment.

  • Good Faith Estimate. Under the federal No Surprises Act, if you are paying for care yourself, you have the right to a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges before your visit. For ongoing counseling, it can cover the sessions you expect over the coming months. If your final bill is $400 or more above the estimate, you can dispute it.
  • Superbill. We can give you a superbill, an itemized receipt with the codes insurers use. Some insurance plans reimburse part of the cost of out-of-network counseling. Check your plan's out-of-network mental health benefits before you start.
  • HSA and FSA. Health savings and flexible spending funds are often usable for counseling. Confirm with your plan administrator.

For more on paying for care across our services, see self-pay pricing.

Honest Limits

What Counseling Here Is Not

We want you to get the right level of care, even if that is somewhere else. Our counseling service is outpatient and by appointment. It is not set up for:

  • Emergencies or crisis care. If you are thinking about harming yourself or someone else, call or text 988 or call 911 now.
  • Situations that need more support than weekly sessions, such as psychosis or a severe eating disorder.
  • Substance use treatment programs.
  • Court-ordered, custody or disability evaluations, psychological testing, or emotional support animal letters.

If any of these apply, we will help you find a better fit. For residents of Stanislaus County, Stanislaus County Behavioral Health and Recovery Services provides specialty mental health care, and the SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) offers free, confidential referrals around the clock.

Questions

Counseling: Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a referral to see a counselor?

No. You can call us directly. A short screening call comes first to make sure our outpatient service fits what you need. The call covers what you would like help with, whether you prefer in-person or telehealth sessions, and a few safety questions. If you need a different kind of care, we will tell you honestly and point you toward it.

Do you take insurance for counseling?

Counseling is offered on a self-pay basis. We can provide a superbill you may submit to your plan for possible out-of-network reimbursement. Call us to ask about current pricing and payment options. Self-pay clients also receive a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges before starting.

How much does a therapy session cost if I pay myself?

Self-pay rates vary widely across California. We review our current pricing with you before your first session, and you receive a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges. It is part of the paperwork you get before you start, along with intake forms and a consent form. If you have questions about the estimate, ask us before your first appointment.

Can I do counseling online?

Yes. Telehealth sessions use a secure video platform. You need to be in California during each session. If you choose online sessions, you sign a telehealth consent before you start. In-person sessions are also available at our Modesto practice. The short screening call is a good time to tell us which you prefer.

What if I am in crisis?

Call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or call 911. Our counseling service is not an emergency service, and messages left after hours are not monitored. If you are in crisis, please reach out right away. Our service is outpatient counseling for adults, and the screening call helps make sure it is the right fit for you.

Do your counselors prescribe medication?

No. Counseling here is talk therapy. Medication questions should go to a physician, such as your primary care doctor. Counseling is often used alongside medical care, so you can see a counselor and a physician at the same time. Your counselor can talk with you about how the two fit together, and you do not need a diagnosis to start.
