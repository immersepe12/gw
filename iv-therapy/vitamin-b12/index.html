Vitamin B12: Deficiency, Shots and IV

Vitamin B12 helps your body make red blood cells and keep nerves healthy. If a blood test shows you are low, B12 shots or high-dose pills are the standard treatments. B12 is also in our Energy Drip, but for people with normal levels, extra B12 has no proven benefit.

Generations Wellness Medical Practice · Medical director Dr. Rajwinder Singh Bahia, MD · Updated October 14, 2026

The Basics

What Vitamin B12 Does

Vitamin B12, also called cobalamin, is a vitamin your body needs to make healthy red blood cells, make DNA, and keep your nerves and brain working normally. It also plays a role in normal energy metabolism. Your body cannot make B12, so it comes from food: meat, fish, shellfish, eggs, milk and fortified foods such as some cereals. According to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, most adults need about 2.4 micrograms a day.

To absorb B12 from food, your stomach needs acid and a protein called intrinsic factor. When either is missing, B12 can run low even if you eat plenty of it.

Deficiency

Who Gets Low on B12, and the Signs

People more likely to be low

  • Older adults, who often make less stomach acid
  • People with pernicious anemia, an autoimmune condition that blocks intrinsic factor
  • People who have had weight-loss surgery or part of the stomach or small intestine removed
  • People with Crohn's disease or celiac disease
  • Vegans and some vegetarians who don't use fortified foods or supplements
  • People who take metformin or acid-reducing medicines for a long time

Possible signs

Low B12 can cause tiredness, weakness, pale skin, shortness of breath, a sore tongue, and numbness or tingling in the hands and feet. Some people notice memory trouble, mood changes or balance problems. These symptoms have many other causes too, so they are a reason to get checked, not a diagnosis.

How it is tested

A simple blood test measures your B12 level. Your physician may also check a complete blood count, and sometimes methylmalonic acid or homocysteine, which can show a deficiency more clearly when the B12 level is borderline. Testing first matters: guessing can hide a problem that needs a different treatment.

For a confirmed deficiency, B12 shots or high-dose pills are what most guidelines use. An IV drip is not a better way to fix a deficiency. B12 is one of the ingredients in our Energy Drip, but if your B12 is normal, there is no good evidence that extra B12 raises energy. Your body stores B12 in the liver and passes extra in urine.

Why testing comes before treatment

Taking B12 before a blood test can make the result look normal even when there is an underlying problem, such as pernicious anemia, that needs lifelong treatment and follow-up. Folic acid can also hide the anemia of B12 deficiency while nerve damage continues. That is why a physician usually wants to test first, find out why you are low, and then choose the right treatment and how often to recheck.

How long treatment lasts

It depends on the cause. If low B12 came from diet, it may be corrected and then kept up with food or a daily supplement. If it came from an absorption problem, treatment is often long-term. Your physician will explain the plan and when to repeat blood tests.

Side effects of B12

B12 is generally well tolerated. Shots can cause soreness at the injection site. Some people notice mild diarrhea, itching or a rash. Rarely, serious allergic reactions occur. People with Leber's disease, an inherited eye condition, should not take the cyanocobalamin form of B12. Tell us about all your medicines at screening.

Questions

Vitamin B12: Frequently Asked Questions

Do B12 shots give you energy?

If you are deficient, treating it may ease tiredness caused by the deficiency. If your levels are normal, there is no good evidence extra B12 raises energy.

Is a B12 shot or IV better?

For a diagnosed deficiency, shots into the muscle or high-dose pills are the standard treatments. An IV is not a better way to correct low B12.

How do I know if I'm low on B12?

A blood test is the only way to know. Symptoms like tiredness or tingling have many causes, so talk with a physician about testing.

Can you take too much B12?

NIH has not set an upper limit because B12 has low potential for harm, but side effects and allergic reactions can still happen. More is not better if your level is already normal.
