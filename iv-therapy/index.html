The Basics

What IV Therapy Is, and How We Approach It

IV (intravenous) therapy delivers fluid, and sometimes vitamins or other nutrients, through a small catheter placed in a vein, usually in the arm or hand. Hospitals have used IV fluids for decades to treat dehydration and to give medicines. Over the last several years, "IV drips" have also become popular in lounges and spas, often advertised with big promises.

We take a more careful approach. At Generations Wellness Medical Practice, IV therapy is a medical service. It is ordered by a physician, it is given in our outpatient clinic by licensed clinicians, and it starts with a health screening. If an IV is not a good fit for you, we will say so and talk about other options.

What we will and won't say about IV drips

You may have seen IV drips marketed for immunity, hangovers, glowing skin or weight loss. We do not make those claims, because the scientific evidence does not support them for IV vitamins in people without a deficiency. The U.S. Federal Trade Commission requires that health claims be backed by competent and reliable scientific evidence, and it has taken action against IV drip businesses that made unsupported claims. We would rather give you honest information and let you decide.

What we can say: an IV gives fluid directly into the bloodstream, which can help with hydration. The vitamins in our blend are nutrients your body uses every day. Whether an IV makes sense for you is a medical decision we make with you, not a menu choice.

Honest Answers

What the Evidence Shows About IV Vitamins

The research picture is simple to state: evidence that IV vitamins help people who are not deficient is limited. Most healthy adults get enough B vitamins from food, and the body flushes out extra water-soluble vitamins in urine.

Where IV or injected nutrients have a clear medical role

  • Dehydration. IV fluids are a standard way to restore fluid when someone cannot drink enough.
  • Documented vitamin B12 deficiency, especially when the body cannot absorb B12 from food, such as with pernicious anemia or after some stomach surgeries. Injections are the usual treatment in that case, and high-dose oral B12 also works for many people, according to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements.

Where evidence is weak

For general energy, focus, skin, immunity or recovery, there are few good-quality studies of IV vitamin blends, and they do not show consistent benefits. The well-known "Myers' cocktail" has been studied only in small trials. We explain more in our Energy Drip guide.

If you feel tired all the time, that is worth a real medical look. Fatigue can come from anemia, thyroid problems, sleep issues, depression, medications and many other causes. A physician visit and, when appropriate, blood tests may find something an IV would only cover up.

Before Any IV

A Health Screening Comes First

  • Health history. We ask about heart, kidney and liver conditions, pregnancy, allergies and past reactions to IVs or vitamins.
  • Medication review. Some medicines and supplements interact with vitamins or affect fluid balance.
  • Vital signs. Blood pressure, heart rate and how you are feeling that day.
  • A physician order. An IV is given only when a physician has reviewed your screening and decided it is appropriate.
  • Monitoring during the drip, with a clinician nearby who can slow or stop the IV if you feel unwell.

Common side effects of any IV include soreness, bruising or a small bump where the needle went in, a cool feeling in the arm, and lightheadedness. Less often, the vein can become irritated or infected, or a person can have an allergic reaction. Read the full list in our IV therapy cost and safety guide.

Safety

Who Should Not Have IV Therapy

IV therapy is not right for everyone. Extra fluid and certain nutrients can be risky for some people. We generally will not give an elective IV, or will need input from your own doctor first, if you have:

  • Heart failure or other conditions where extra fluid can build up
  • Kidney disease or reduced kidney function
  • Serious liver disease
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding, unless your obstetric clinician agrees
  • A known allergy to any ingredient, or a past reaction to IV vitamins
  • Uncontrolled high blood pressure
  • Certain rare conditions, such as Leber's disease (an inherited eye condition), where some forms of vitamin B12 are not advised

If you have a fever, chest pain, trouble breathing, confusion or severe vomiting, an IV drip at a clinic is not the right place. Call 911 or go to an emergency room.

Questions

IV Therapy in Modesto: Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a doctor's order for IV therapy?

Yes. At our practice, every IV is ordered by a physician after a health screening. You do not need to bring an order from another doctor; the screening happens here.

How long does an IV drip take?

A drip commonly takes about 30 to 60 minutes. Plan extra time for your screening, especially on your first visit.

Does IV therapy actually work?

IV fluids help with hydration, and B12 is an established treatment for documented deficiency. For people without a deficiency, evidence for IV vitamin blends is limited, and we will tell you that plainly.

Is IV therapy safe?

For screened adults it is generally low risk, but no IV is free of risk. Bruising, soreness and lightheadedness are common; vein irritation, infection and allergic reactions are less common. That is why screening comes first.
