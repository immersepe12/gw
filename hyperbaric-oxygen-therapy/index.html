At a Glance

Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy at Our Modesto Practice

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy means breathing close to 100% medical-grade oxygen while the air pressure around you is raised above normal. Under that pressure, far more oxygen dissolves into your blood plasma than at normal air pressure, so it can reach tissue where circulation is poor. That extra oxygen may support the body's own healing response in specific, well-studied conditions.

At Generations Wellness, you rest inside a hard-shell monoplace chamber: a clear, rigid, single-person medical chamber. Your physician prescribes the pressure and the length of each session. Staff stay in contact with you for the whole session, and you can talk with them through the chamber's communication system.

  • Physician-directed: care is led by medical director Dr. Rajwinder Singh Bahia, MD.
  • Hard-shell chambers built for clinical treatment pressures, not low-pressure soft "mild" chambers.
  • FDA-cleared indications only. We tell you plainly when HBOT is not the right tool.
  • Outpatient, by appointment in downtown Modesto, with wound care and other services in the same practice.

Who It Is For

Conditions Hyperbaric Chambers Are FDA-Cleared For

The FDA clears hyperbaric chambers for a defined list of uses, and the Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical Society (UHMS) publishes a similar list of accepted indications. HBOT is used alongside standard treatment for these conditions, never instead of it. Your physician decides whether your situation fits and whether HBOT is safe for you.

Outpatient indications we assess for

  • Diabetic foot ulcers that have not healed with good standard wound care
  • Selected non-healing wounds, including problem wounds with poor oxygen supply
  • Delayed radiation injury, including osteoradionecrosis of the jaw and radiation cystitis or proctitis
  • Compromised skin grafts and flaps
  • Chronic refractory osteomyelitis (bone infection that has not responded to treatment)
  • Sudden sensorineural hearing loss, ideally started within the first two weeks alongside steroids

Emergency indications (hospital care first)

Some cleared uses are emergencies: carbon monoxide poisoning, decompression sickness, gas embolism and other emergency indications, necrotizing soft tissue infections, crush injury and severe thermal burns. Generations Wellness is an outpatient practice and does not provide emergency hyperbaric treatment. In an emergency, call 911.

Hyperbaric oxygen is not an FDA-cleared treatment for autism, stroke, brain injury, long COVID, Alzheimer's disease, cancer or anti-aging. If you have read about these uses, our HBOT research overview explains what the studies do and do not show. See the full list of conditions HBOT is cleared for.

The Equipment

Our Hard-Shell Monoplace Chambers

Not every "hyperbaric chamber" delivers the same treatment. Medical HBOT protocols for cleared conditions commonly use pressures of about 2.0 to 2.4 atmospheres absolute (ATA) with close to 100% oxygen. Soft-sided inflatable chambers are usually limited to about 1.3 ATA and are FDA-cleared only for acute mountain sickness. UHMS does not recommend low-pressure fabric chambers for other medical uses.

A monoplace chamber treats one person at a time. You lie on a padded stretcher, breathe normally without a mask, and can see out through the clear shell. Because the chamber is oxygen-rich, there are strict safety rules about clothing and items inside, which our team walks you through before your first session.

For a deeper comparison, read hard-shell vs. soft-shell hyperbaric chambers, 1.3 ATA vs. 2.0 ATA treatment pressure and home chambers vs. clinic HBOT. The science of how pressure moves oxygen into tissue is covered on how hyperbaric oxygen therapy works.

Your Course

What a Course of HBOT Looks Like

  1. Physician assessment

    We review your diagnosis, records, medications, and ear, sinus and lung health. If HBOT is not appropriate, we tell you why and what else may help.

  2. Your treatment plan

    Your physician sets the pressure, session length and number of sessions, and coordinates with the doctors already treating you.

  3. Sessions

    A session typically runs about 90 to 120 minutes including pressurization. Many courses are 20 to 40 sessions, usually on weekdays, depending on the condition.

  4. Progress reviews

    Your response is tracked through the course, and the plan is adjusted or stopped if it is not helping or causes problems.

The start of a session feels like a plane descending: pressure builds in your ears, and you clear it by swallowing or yawning. Then most people rest, nap or listen to something. Safety

Side Effects and Who Should Not Have HBOT

HBOT is generally well tolerated when patients are screened carefully, but it is a medical treatment with real side effects. The most common is ear or sinus pressure, which can lead to ear barotrauma if pressure is not cleared. Others include temporary changes in near vision during a long course, fatigue, and anxiety in an enclosed space. Rare but serious risks include oxygen toxicity seizures and lung injury.

Some people should not have HBOT, or need extra evaluation first. An untreated collapsed lung (pneumothorax) is the main absolute contraindication. Certain lung diseases, some chemotherapy drugs, recent ear or sinus surgery, uncontrolled fever and some implanted devices need a careful physician review. Read who should not use a hyperbaric chamber and the full list of hyperbaric oxygen therapy side effects. If small spaces worry you, see our guide to ear pressure and claustrophobia in HBOT.

Cost and Coverage

What HBOT Costs and How Coverage Works

The cost of a course depends on how many sessions your physician recommends. Medicare and many private plans may cover HBOT for certain cleared conditions when specific criteria are met, such as those in Medicare's National Coverage Determination 20.29, and usually do not cover it for uses outside that list.

Call us to ask about current pricing and payment options; we review costs with you before treatment. To understand typical national price ranges and the rules insurers use, read how much hyperbaric oxygen therapy costs, does insurance cover HBOT and does Medicare cover hyperbaric oxygen therapy.

Quick Answers

Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy in Modesto: FAQ

Is there a hyperbaric chamber near me in Modesto?

Yes. Generations Wellness Medical Practice has hard-shell monoplace hyperbaric chambers at 1801 H Street, Suite C-1, in downtown Modesto. Visits are by appointment; call (369) 222-0979 or (209) 809-0022.

Do I need a referral for HBOT?

You can call us directly. Whether or not you have a referral, a physician assessment here is required before any HBOT session. If your doctor referred you, we coordinate with them.

What does an HBOT session feel like?

Most people compare the start to an airplane landing: ear pressure you clear by swallowing or yawning. Once at pressure, you rest. Some people feel tired afterward.

How many HBOT sessions will I need?

It depends on the condition and your response. Many courses for wounds or radiation injury are 20 to 40 sessions; your physician sets the plan and reviews it as you go.

Can HBOT help with conditions that are not on the cleared list?

The evidence for most other uses is limited or mixed, and the FDA has warned against claims for them. We will discuss the research honestly, but we provide HBOT for FDA-cleared indications.
