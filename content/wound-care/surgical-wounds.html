The Basics

What Normal Incision Healing Looks Like

After surgery, it is normal for an incision to be a little red, swollen and tender for the first few days. The edges usually seal within a couple of days, and the skin closes over the next one to three weeks. Mild itching as it heals is common. Deeper healing and scar strengthening continue for months.

Signs that healing is not on track include:

  • Redness that spreads or grows after the first few days
  • Increasing pain instead of gradual improvement
  • Thick, cloudy, yellow or green drainage, or a bad smell
  • Edges that pull apart, or a gap that gets wider
  • Fever or chills
  • An incision still open after several weeks

Some incisions are left open on purpose to heal from the bottom up. If that's your situation, your surgeon will give you specific care instructions, and slower closure is expected.

Causes

Why a Surgical Wound May Heal Slowly

  • Infection (a surgical site infection), the most common cause
  • Diabetes, especially with high blood sugar
  • Smoking or vaping, which reduces blood flow to the skin
  • Obesity, which adds tension on the incision and can reduce blood supply to fatty tissue
  • Poor nutrition, especially low protein
  • Medicines such as steroids, some immune-suppressing drugs and some cancer treatments
  • Past radiation to the area (see radiation tissue injury)
  • Strain on the incision from coughing, lifting or straining too soon
  • Fluid collections under the skin, such as a seroma or hematoma

At Home

Incision Care at Home

Always follow the instructions from your surgical team first. General incision care tips include:

  • Wash your hands before and after touching the incision or dressing.
  • Keep the dressing clean and dry, and change it as directed.
  • Don't soak the incision in a bath, hot tub or pool until your surgeon says it's okay.
  • Don't put creams, powders or home remedies on it unless told to.
  • Leave skin glue or tape strips alone; they usually fall off on their own.
  • Support the incision with a pillow when you cough or sneeze if it's on your belly or chest.
  • Avoid heavy lifting and straining for as long as your surgeon advises.
  • Eat enough protein, drink fluids, and if you smoke, ask for help stopping.

Check the incision once a day in good light. Taking a photo every day or two can help you notice changes and show them to your care team.

When It Opens

Wound Dehiscence: When an Incision Opens

Dehiscence means a surgical wound that has partly or fully reopened. It most often happens in the first one to two weeks after surgery, and abdominal incisions are the most common site. You might feel a pulling or popping sensation, or notice new pink fluid soaking the dressing.

What to do

  1. Cover the wound with a clean, damp dressing or cloth.
  2. Call your surgeon right away.
  3. If the opening is large, you can see tissue underneath, or bleeding is heavy, call 911 and lie still.

Small openings are often managed with wound care and allowed to heal from the bottom up. Larger ones may need a return to surgery. Your surgeon decides which is right.

Is hyperbaric oxygen used?

For most surgical wounds, no. HBOT is used only for FDA-cleared indications. Two can apply after surgery: a compromised skin graft or flap, and wounds in tissue damaged by past radiation. Your physician decides whether HBOT is appropriate.

Questions

Surgical Wounds: Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a surgical incision take to heal?

Most closed incisions seal at the surface within one to three weeks, though deeper healing takes months. Ask your surgeon what to expect for your procedure.

How do I know if my incision is infected?

Watch for spreading redness, warmth, increasing pain, pus or a bad smell, and fever. Call your surgeon the same day if you notice these.

What should I do if my incision opens?

Cover it with a clean, damp dressing and call your surgeon. Call 911 for a large opening, visible tissue underneath or heavy bleeding.

Can I see a wound clinic without my surgeon?

You can call us, but we will want to work with your surgeon, especially in the first weeks after surgery, so your care stays coordinated.

Is it normal for a surgical incision to itch while healing?

Yes, mild itching is common as an incision heals, because skin and small nerves are repairing. Try not to scratch, keep the area clean and follow your surgeon's care instructions. Itching that comes with spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus, a bad smell or fever is different: those can be signs of infection and need same-day medical care.
