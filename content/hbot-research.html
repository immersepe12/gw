Definitions

Cleared, Investigational, or Marketed: Three Very Different Words

FDA-cleared

The FDA clears hyperbaric chambers as medical devices for specific uses. Those uses include air or gas embolism, carbon monoxide poisoning, decompression sickness, crush injuries, certain non-healing diabetic wounds, delayed radiation injury, compromised skin grafts and flaps, and sudden hearing loss, among others. "Cleared" is tied to the indication, not to HBOT in general. That is why describing HBOT as a blanket "approved" treatment is misleading. You can see the full list on our page about the FDA-cleared conditions list.

Investigational

An investigational use is one that researchers are studying in trials. Some of those trials report encouraging results. But the use is not cleared by the FDA and is not on the Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical Society (UHMS) list of recognized indications. "Being studied" is not the same as "shown to work."

Marketed

Some clinics sell HBOT for conditions regardless of the evidence. The FDA has warned about this directly. Its consumer update says hyperbaric chambers have not been cleared for, among others:

  • Cancer, autism, Lyme disease and Alzheimer's disease
  • Stroke, brain injury, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis and Parkinson's disease
  • Depression and migraine
  • Heart disease, asthma and HIV/AIDS
  • Sports injuries and COVID-19

The FDA also recommends that anyone considering HBOT receive it under a doctor's care at an accredited facility.

A 90-Second Guide

How to Read an HBOT Study

You don't need a medical degree to judge a headline about hyperbaric oxygen. Ask these five questions about any study you read, including the ones on our research pages.

  1. Was there a sham or control group? A sham group sits in a chamber that feels similar but delivers little or no extra pressure or oxygen. Without one, you can't tell whether HBOT caused the change or whether time, attention and expectation did.
  2. How many people took part? Trials of 25 to 80 people can hint at an effect. They rarely settle a question on their own.
  3. What pressure was used? "Mild" chambers run around 1.3 ATA (atmospheres absolute). Medical studies usually use 1.5 to 2.4 ATA. A result at one pressure tells you little about another.
  4. What was measured? A change in a lab marker or a brain scan is not the same as feeling better, working again, or living longer.
  5. Has it been repeated? A finding from one research center needs confirming at others before anyone can rely on it.

One lesson from the brain-injury research is worth knowing. In several military trials, people improved in both the HBOT group and the sham group, by similar amounts. "Participants improved" is not the same as "HBOT worked." The TBI and concussion research page explains that debate in detail.

Deep Dives

The Research Pages

Each page has a full studies table, a section on what the research does not show, and sources. Here is the one-line status for each.

  • HBOT for long COVID. One positive sham-controlled trial of 73 people (40 sessions) and one 80-person trial (10 sessions) that found no difference from placebo. Not cleared.
  • HBOT for TBI and concussion. Sham-controlled military trials found little or no lasting HBOT-versus-sham difference; smaller trials without a sham group reported gains. Not cleared.
  • HBOT for stroke recovery. No benefit shown in acute stroke (Cochrane review of 11 trials); one chronic-stroke crossover trial without a sham reported gains; stroke guidelines do not recommend it. Not cleared.
  • HBOT after surgery. Cleared only for compromised grafts, flaps and certain non-healing wounds. Evidence for routine elective recovery is small and low quality.
  • HBOT for athletes. Mixed. A Cochrane review found no benefit for soreness or sprains; newer small trials report faster recovery of lab markers. The FDA lists sports injuries as not cleared.
  • HBOT and anti-aging. Single-center studies of blood markers and thinking skills in healthy older adults; no sham group and no clinical-outcome data. Not cleared.

Questions

HBOT Research: Frequently Asked Questions

Is HBOT FDA approved?

Hyperbaric chambers are FDA-cleared devices for specific indications, such as carbon monoxide poisoning, certain diabetic foot ulcers and delayed radiation injury. HBOT is not cleared as a general therapy, and it is not cleared for the conditions in this section.

What conditions is HBOT not approved for?

Any condition outside the FDA-cleared and UHMS-recognized list, including autism, cancer, Lyme disease, multiple sclerosis, Alzheimer's, stroke, brain injury, long COVID and anti-aging, regardless of how it is marketed elsewhere.

Are there other conditions that may benefit from HBOT?

Some are being studied, with mixed results. "Being studied" is not the same as "shown to work," and none of them is a cleared indication.

Does hyperbaric oxygen feed cancer?

A 2012 review in Targeted Oncology (Moen and Stuhr) found no evidence that HBOT stimulates tumor growth or increases recurrence. HBOT is used in cancer survivors for delayed radiation tissue injury, a cleared indication, but it is not a cancer treatment, and your physician reviews your cancer history before any course.

Does the VA cover HBOT for PTSD or TBI?

Not as standard care. A VA evidence review found no short-term benefit over sham. Some veterans have accessed HBOT through pilot or state-funded programs or clinical trials.
