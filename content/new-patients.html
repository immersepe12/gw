Referrals: when you need one

For most services, no. You can call us directly. A few things to know:

  • Physical therapy: under California law, as of 2026, you can see a physical therapist without a referral for up to 45 calendar days or 12 visits, whichever comes first. After that, a physician must sign your plan of care, and having a physician in the same practice makes that step simpler. Rules can change.
  • Hyperbaric oxygen therapy and IV therapy start with a physician assessment or health screening here, whether or not you have a referral.
  • Your health plan may have its own rules. Some plans require a referral for their coverage, so check with your plan if that matters to you.

If another doctor is referring you, they are welcome to send records ahead of your visit.

Checklist

What to Bring to Your First Visit

  • A photo ID. For a child, bring the parent's or guardian's ID.
  • A list of your medicines, including vitamins and supplements, with doses.
  • Allergies and past medical history, including surgeries and hospital stays.
  • Relevant records: imaging reports, recent lab results, wound care notes, school evaluations or IEPs for children, and any referral letter.
  • Your questions and goals, written down so nothing is forgotten.
  • Comfortable clothing for therapy visits where you will move, and a way to pay. Call us to ask about current pricing and payment options; we review costs with you before treatment.

If you are coming for a child, bring any notes from their pediatrician or teachers, and a favorite small toy or snack can help them settle. Please arrive 10 to 15 minutes early to finish paperwork. See self-pay pricing for more on paying for care.

By Service

What to Expect at Your First Visit, by Service

Every service starts with an assessment by a licensed clinician before a treatment plan is made, and your clinicians share one care plan if you use more than one service.

  • Hyperbaric oxygen therapy: a physician assessment of your condition and a safety screening of ears, lungs and medicines. HBOT is offered for FDA-cleared indications. Read what an HBOT session involves.
  • Wound care: a review of your wound, health history and current treatment, and a care plan.
  • IV therapy: a health screening first. IV therapy is physician-ordered and given in the clinic only if it is appropriate for you.
  • Counseling: a short screening phone call, intake forms, then a first session that is an assessment. Available in person or by telehealth for adults.
  • Physical therapy: an evaluation of movement, strength and pain, and a plan with exercises to start.
  • Occupational therapy: an evaluation of the daily tasks that are hard for you and a plan to work on them.
  • Pediatric speech therapy and pediatric therapy: a play-based evaluation with you in the room, plus your questions and goals for your child.
  • Pain management: a physician evaluation of your pain, history and past treatments, with a non-opioid-first plan.
