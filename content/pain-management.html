How We Work

A Physician-Led, Non-Opioid-First Approach to Pain

Pain that lasts for months changes how you sleep, work, move and feel. It rarely has one simple fix. Most people do best with a plan that combines several tools: the right medicine at the right dose, movement and exercise, help with daily tasks, and support for the stress that long-term pain brings.

That is the idea behind pain management at Generations Wellness Medical Practice. Your care is led by a physician. A licensed clinician assesses you before any treatment plan is made. And because physical therapy, occupational therapy and counseling are part of the same outpatient practice, your plan can be shared across those services instead of scattered across different offices.

What "non-opioid-first" means

The CDC's 2022 clinical practice guideline for prescribing opioids for pain says non-opioid treatments are at least as effective as opioids for many common types of chronic pain, including low back pain, neck pain and osteoarthritis. It recommends that clinicians make the most of non-drug and non-opioid treatments first. We follow that approach. It does not mean pain is ignored or that every medicine is off the table. It means we start with the options that carry less risk and build from there, based on your history and goals.

Function, not just a pain score

A pain score from 0 to 10 is useful, but it is not the whole story. We also ask what pain is stopping you from doing. Walking to the mailbox, lifting a grandchild, sleeping through the night or getting back to work are real goals we can track. Progress often shows up there first.

Common Question

What Does a Pain Management Doctor Do?

A pain management physician looks for the cause of your pain, rules out serious problems, and coordinates a plan to reduce pain and improve how you function. Some pain doctors focus on procedures such as injections. Others, like our physician-led team, focus on evaluation, medication management and coordinated therapy, and refer to procedure specialists when that is the right next step.

At our practice, pain management care can include:

  • A physician evaluation. Your history, past treatments, current medicines, an exam, and a review of any imaging or records you bring.
  • Non-opioid medication management where appropriate. Reviewing what you take, adjusting doses, and checking for side effects and drug interactions.
  • Coordination with physical therapy. Exercise and movement plans from licensed physical therapists in the same practice.
  • Coordination with occupational therapy. Practical ways to manage work, home tasks and self-care with less pain.
  • Counseling for living with pain. Talk therapy with licensed counselors to help with the stress, sleep problems and low mood that often come with chronic pain.
  • Referral to specialists. When imaging, surgery or an interventional procedure may help, we refer you and share your records with your permission.

Conditions

Types of Pain We Help People Manage

Each guide below explains the condition, what usually helps, and when to get urgent care.

  • Chronic back pain — low back pain that has lasted more than three months.
  • Sciatica — pain that travels from the low back down the leg, often from an irritated nerve root.
  • Arthritis pain — joint pain from osteoarthritis in the knees, hips and hands, and other forms of arthritis.
  • Neuropathy — burning, tingling or numb feet and hands, including diabetic nerve pain.
  • Fibromyalgia — widespread pain with fatigue and sleep problems.
  • Headaches and migraines — frequent or chronic headaches that disrupt daily life.

Neck, shoulder and knee pain are often best started with physical therapy. See our guides to neck pain, shoulder pain and knee pain.

How long does pain have to last to be "chronic"?

Doctors usually call pain chronic when it lasts longer than about three months, past the normal time for tissues to heal. The CDC estimates that about one in five U.S. adults lives with chronic pain. Chronic pain is common, it is real, and it deserves a careful plan.

A note on opioids

We do not position our practice around opioid prescriptions. If you already take an opioid prescribed elsewhere, tell us. Stopping suddenly can be dangerous, and any change should be planned with the prescriber. In California, prescribers who write for controlled medicines check the state's prescription drug monitoring program, as state law requires.

Where hyperbaric oxygen therapy fits

Our practice also offers hyperbaric oxygen therapy, but only for FDA-cleared indications such as certain non-healing wounds and radiation injuries. HBOT is not a cleared treatment for back pain, arthritis, fibromyalgia, neuropathy or migraine, and we do not offer it for those. See the conditions HBOT is cleared for.

Safety First

When Pain Is an Emergency

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room if you have pain with any of these:

  • New loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin or inner thighs
  • Sudden weakness in an arm or leg, or trouble walking
  • Fever with back pain, especially with a history of IV drug use, a weak immune system or recent surgery
  • Pain after a major fall, car crash or other trauma
  • Chest pain, or a sudden, severe "worst ever" headache
  • A headache with fever, stiff neck, confusion, vision loss or trouble speaking

See a doctor soon (not an emergency) for pain with unexplained weight loss, a history of cancer, night pain that doesn't ease with rest, or pain that keeps getting worse.

Questions

Pain Management: Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a referral to see you for pain?

Call us to check what applies to your situation. Bringing records from your other doctors helps us see the full picture and avoid repeating tests.

Do you prescribe opioids?

Our approach is non-opioid-first, in line with CDC guidance. Medication decisions are made by a physician after an evaluation, based on your history, risks and goals.

Do you do injections or nerve blocks?

No. When a procedure may help, we refer you to a specialist and keep coordinating the rest of your care.

How much does pain management cost without insurance?

Costs depend on your plan and the services involved. Call us to ask about current pricing and payment options; we review costs with you before treatment.

Can I keep seeing my own doctor?

Yes. With your permission, we share updates with your other doctors so everyone is working from the same plan.
