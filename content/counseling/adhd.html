Understanding ADHD

What ADHD Looks Like in Adults

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a condition that affects attention, activity level and impulse control. The National Institute of Mental Health notes that symptoms begin in childhood but often continue into adulthood, and many adults were never diagnosed as kids.

In adults, ADHD often looks less like bouncing off the walls and more like this:

  • Losing track of time and running late, again.
  • Starting many projects and finishing few.
  • Putting off boring tasks like bills or paperwork until they become emergencies.
  • Misplacing keys, phone or wallet most days.
  • Restlessness, interrupting, or blurting things out.
  • Big emotional reactions and frustration that flare fast.

Years of missed deadlines and "you're not trying hard enough" can leave adults with ADHD feeling ashamed, anxious or low. That emotional weight is a big part of what counseling addresses.

Know the Roles

Diagnosis and Medication Stay With Your Doctor

Our counseling service provides talk therapy and skills coaching. We do not offer ADHD diagnostic testing, and counselors do not prescribe.

  • For a diagnosis, talk with your primary care doctor or a psychiatrist, or ask for a referral to a clinician who does ADHD evaluations. The NIMH notes that an evaluation looks at symptoms across settings and rules out other explanations, such as sleep problems, anxiety or depression.
  • For medication, talk with a physician or psychiatrist. Many adults use medicine and counseling together.

You do not need a formal diagnosis to start counseling. If you suspect ADHD, a counselor can help you organize your history and questions before you see a doctor, and work on practical strategies in the meantime.

Approaches

CBT and Skills Coaching for ADHD

The NIMH lists psychotherapy and skills training among the common treatments for adult ADHD. Cognitive behavioral therapy adapted for ADHD is one of the more studied approaches. It does not aim to change how your brain is wired. It helps you build outside structure that works with it.

Time and planning

You practice one simple planner system, break tasks into tiny first steps, use timers and alarms, and plan your day around when your focus is strongest.

Getting started on hard tasks

Counselors help you spot the thoughts that feed procrastination, like "I'll do it when I feel like it," and try tricks such as the five-minute start or body doubling.

Organization at home and work

A landing spot for keys, a weekly reset, reminders on autopay. Small systems remove daily friction.

Emotions and relationships

Sessions often cover managing frustration, rejection sensitivity and conflict with partners or coworkers who misread ADHD as not caring. Some clients bring a partner to a session.

Progress depends on practice between sessions and on your goals. Results differ from person to person, and your counselor reviews what is working along the way.

Teens and Kids

ADHD Therapy for Teens

Teens with ADHD often struggle with homework, missed assignments, phones and friction at home. Skills-based counseling and parent coaching can help. Services for younger clients depend on clinician availability, so please call to ask, and see our guide to counseling for teens and children.

For younger children whose ADHD shows up as trouble with handwriting, sitting still, routines or sensory needs, our occupational therapy for kids with ADHD takes a hands-on approach. Students may also be eligible for school supports through a 504 plan or IEP; ask your school district.

Questions

ADHD Counseling: Frequently Asked Questions

Can you diagnose me with ADHD?

Our counseling service does not provide ADHD diagnostic testing. For an evaluation, talk with your primary care doctor or a psychiatrist, or ask for a referral to a clinician who specializes in ADHD assessments. You can still start counseling while you wait, and work on practical skills for focus, time and organization.

Does therapy help ADHD if I already take medication?

Many adults find the two work well together. Medicine can make focus easier, while counseling builds the habits, systems and coping skills that medicine alone does not teach. Counselors do not prescribe or adjust medication; keep those conversations with your physician or psychiatrist, and let your counselor know what you take.

What is CBT for ADHD?

It is a version of cognitive behavioral therapy adapted for adults with ADHD. It focuses on concrete skills such as planning, breaking down tasks, reducing distractions and managing procrastination, plus working on discouraging thoughts built up over years. It is structured and practical, with short practice between sessions to help the skills stick.

Is ADHD counseling available by telehealth?

Yes, by secure video while you are in California. Many adults with ADHD like telehealth because it removes travel time and makes it easier to keep appointments. Your counselor can help you set up reminders so sessions do not slip, and can explain how a video visit works before your first session.

My child has ADHD. Where should we start?

Start with your pediatrician, who can evaluate ADHD or refer you, and contact your school district about a 504 plan or IEP evaluation. For hands-on help with routines, handwriting or sensory needs, see our occupational therapy page for kids with ADHD. For teen counseling, call to ask about current availability.
