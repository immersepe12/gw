Counseling for Younger Clients at Our Practice

Our counseling service is built first for adults. Whether we can see a teen or child depends on whether a licensed counselor with training and experience in that age group has availability. We would rather tell you that honestly than book your child with someone who is not the right fit.

What to Watch For

Signs Your Child or Teen May Need Support

Every child has bad days, and moodiness is part of growing up. The CDC and NIMH suggest paying closer attention when changes last for weeks, show up in more than one setting, or get in the way of school, friendships or family life. Signs include:

  • Persistent sadness, irritability or tearfulness
  • Losing interest in friends, sports or activities they used to enjoy
  • Big changes in sleep or eating
  • Frequent stomachaches or headaches, especially before school
  • Constant worry, fear, or avoiding school or social situations
  • Falling grades or trouble concentrating
  • Pulling away from family, or spending most of their time alone
  • Self-harm, such as cutting, or talk about death or wanting to disappear

Start with your child's pediatrician or family doctor, who can check for medical causes and talk through next steps. If your child talks about suicide or has hurt themselves, call or text 988 or call 911. The 988 Lifeline also supports parents who are worried about a child.

School Stress

School Stress, Social Media and Teens

Teens today juggle grades, college and job pressure, sports, part-time work, friendships and life online. A certain amount of stress is normal. It becomes a concern when it leads to panic before tests, all-night worrying, avoiding school, or a teen saying they feel like they can never do enough.

Things that help at home:

  • Ask open questions and listen more than you fix
  • Protect sleep, including phone-free time before bed
  • Help break big assignments into smaller steps
  • Notice and name effort, not only results
  • Stay in touch with teachers and the school counselor

Counseling for teens often uses the same well-studied approaches as for adults, such as cognitive behavioral therapy, adapted for their age, with parents involved in a way that respects the teen's growing independence. For more on how anxiety counseling works, see counseling for anxiety.

The Law in California

Can a Teen Get Counseling Without a Parent's Consent?

In California, a minor 12 or older may consent to outpatient mental health treatment or counseling on their own if the professional treating them believes the minor is mature enough to participate intelligently. This is set out in California Family Code §6924 and Health and Safety Code §124260.

Before 2024, minors whose care was paid for by Medi-Cal had to meet an extra test, such as being at risk of serious harm or being a victim of abuse, before they could consent on their own. Assembly Bill 665, which took effect July 1, 2024, removed that extra test, so the same standard now applies regardless of who pays.

What this means for families:

  • Under 12: a parent or legal guardian must consent to counseling.
  • 12 to 17: a teen may be able to consent on their own, if the clinician judges them mature enough.
  • Parents are usually involved. The law expects the clinician to involve a parent or guardian unless the clinician decides that would be inappropriate, for example if it could put the teen at risk, and to document that decision.
  • Privacy matters. When a teen consents on their own, much of what they share stays confidential, within legal limits such as safety and mandated reporting.

This describes California law as of 2026. Laws can change, so check the current text or ask a clinician. In practice, most families find counseling works best when parents and teens are both part of the plan.

More Options

Other Places to Find Help in Stanislaus County

If we do not have availability for your child, or you want more options, these routes are worth knowing about:

  • Your child's school. School counselors and psychologists can offer support, and if a mental health condition affects learning, you can ask in writing for an evaluation for special education or a 504 plan at no cost to you.
  • Your health plan. Medi-Cal and commercial plans cover outpatient mental health care for children and teens. Call the member number on your child's card.
  • Stanislaus County Behavioral Health and Recovery Services provides specialty mental health care for more serious needs.
  • Your pediatrician can screen, refer and coordinate care.

Families in Modesto, Ceres, Turlock, Riverbank and Salida have all of these options close to home.

Questions

Teen and Child Counseling: Frequently Asked Questions

Do you see teenagers or children for counseling?

It depends on clinician availability for that age group. Please call us, and we will tell you what we can offer now.

At what age can a child consent to therapy in California?

At 12 or older, a minor may consent to outpatient counseling if the clinician finds them mature enough to participate intelligently. Under 12, a parent or guardian consents.

Will I know what my teen talks about in counseling?

Usually you will be involved in the plan, but details your teen shares are generally kept private, within legal limits around safety. Clinicians explain these limits at the start.

How do I know if my child needs a therapist?

Watch for changes that last weeks and affect school, friends or family life. Your pediatrician is a good first call.

What is AB 665?

A California law, effective July 1, 2024, that applied the same minor-consent standard for outpatient mental health care to all minors 12 and older, including those with Medi-Cal.
