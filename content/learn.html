How We Write

Honest Information, Before You Decide

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy attracts a lot of big promises online. Our guides take the opposite approach. We explain what HBOT is, what a course actually involves, and what the evidence shows, drawing on sources such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, the Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical Society, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services and peer-reviewed research.

You'll notice careful wording throughout: "may support," "is cleared for," "your physician determines." That is deliberate. HBOT is an FDA-cleared treatment for a specific list of conditions, and it is not proven for many others. Where the research is mixed or early, we say so.

If you're new to HBOT, start with how hyperbaric oxygen therapy works and what to expect at your first session. If you have a specific question, the HBOT FAQ answers the most common ones in a few sentences each. Read about how we write and update these guides.

Questions

Learn About HBOT: Frequently Asked Questions

What is hyperbaric oxygen therapy used for?

HBOT is FDA-cleared for a specific list of conditions, including certain diabetic foot ulcers, radiation tissue injury, carbon monoxide poisoning, decompression sickness, some bone infections and compromised skin grafts. It is used alongside standard medical care, not instead of it. For many other uses you may see online, the evidence is limited or still being studied. Our condition guides cover each cleared use.

Is hyperbaric oxygen therapy safe?

HBOT is generally well tolerated when a physician screens and supervises you, but like any treatment it has risks. The most common side effect is ear pressure or discomfort. Less common effects include sinus pain, temporary vision changes and tiredness, and rare risks include oxygen toxicity seizures. A screening before treatment checks for conditions that make HBOT unsafe for you.

How long does a hyperbaric oxygen session last?

A typical session runs about 90 to 120 minutes, including the time it takes to pressurize and depressurize the chamber. The exact time depends on your physician's prescription. Courses for chronic conditions often involve sessions on most weekdays over several weeks. Our guide to how many HBOT sessions people need explains why the number varies by condition.

Does insurance cover hyperbaric oxygen therapy?

Coverage depends on your plan and your condition. Medicare and many private plans may cover HBOT for certain FDA-cleared conditions when their criteria are met, and they usually do not cover investigational uses. Our insurance guides explain how coverage works in general. Call us to ask about current pricing and payment options; we review costs with you before treatment.
