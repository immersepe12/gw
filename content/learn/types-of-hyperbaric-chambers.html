Overview

A Quick Map of Hyperbaric Chamber Types

Hyperbaric chambers are sorted in two useful ways: by how many occupants they hold, which is how fire codes classify them, and by how they are built, which largely decides what pressure they can reach. Put together, you get four types most people will hear about:

  • Hard-shell monoplace: a rigid chamber for one person, often a clear acrylic tube
  • Multiplace: a rigid, room-sized chamber for several people
  • Soft-shell (portable or "mild"): an inflatable fabric chamber, usually around 1.3 ATA
  • Animal (veterinary) chambers: rigid chambers for animals only

Only the first two can deliver hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) as the Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical Society (UHMS) defines it: near-100% oxygen at 1.4 atmospheres absolute (ATA) or higher, with recognized uses commonly treated at 2.0 ATA or more.

Fire Code

NFPA 99 Chamber Classes: A, B and C

In the United States, NFPA 99, the Health Care Facilities Code, sets fire-safety rules for hyperbaric facilities. It groups chambers by occupancy:

  • Class A: human, multiple occupancy. These are multiplace chambers.
  • Class B: human, single occupancy. These are monoplace chambers.
  • Class C: animal occupancy only.

Medical-grade chambers are also built to ASME PVHO-1, the engineering code for pressure vessels that hold people. Soft-shell consumer chambers are generally not designed for these medical-facility codes, which is one of the reasons experts treat them as a separate category.

One Person

Hard-Shell Monoplace Chambers

A monoplace chamber is a rigid tube, usually clear acrylic, that holds one person lying down. It is typically filled and pressurized with near-100% oxygen, so you breathe normally without a mask. Staff watch from outside and talk with you by intercom. This is the most common chamber in outpatient HBOT centers, and it is what our Modesto practice uses.

Several People

Multiplace Chambers

A multiplace chamber is a steel room that holds two or more patients, often with a trained attendant inside. The room is pressurized with air, and each patient breathes oxygen through a hood or mask. Multiplace chambers are found mostly in hospitals and are often used for critical care and emergencies. For a full side-by-side, read monoplace vs. multiplace hyperbaric chambers.

Portable

Soft-Shell, Portable and "Mild" Chambers

Soft-shell chambers are fabric enclosures closed with zippers and inflated by a compressor. They are often sold for home or wellness-studio use and usually reach about 1.3 ATA, so you may see them marketed as "mild HBOT" or "1.3 ATA chambers." Most deliver room air, sometimes with extra oxygen from a concentrator.

The FDA has cleared these low-pressure fabric chambers only for treating acute mountain sickness. The UHMS position is that they do not provide HBOT as medically defined. We cover the details in two pages rather than repeating them here: hard-shell vs. soft-shell chambers and mild vs. medical-grade HBOT. If you are thinking of buying one, also read home chamber vs. clinic.

Checklist

How to Tell Which Type of Chamber You Are In

Marketing names can be confusing. These questions cut through it:

  • Is the chamber rigid or does it zip shut? Zippers and soft walls mean a soft-shell chamber.
  • What pressure is used for treatment? Around 1.3 ATA points to a mild chamber. Recognized medical protocols commonly use 2.0 ATA or more.
  • What are you breathing? Near-100% oxygen, either filling the chamber or through a hood, is HBOT. Room air or a concentrator usually is not.
  • Who is supervising? Medical HBOT is prescribed by a physician after an assessment, with trained staff running each session.
  • What is it FDA-cleared for? Ask for the 510(k) clearance and its listed uses. Our guide to FDA-cleared hyperbaric chambers shows how to check.

Questions

Types of Hyperbaric Chambers: Frequently Asked Questions

What are the three classes of hyperbaric chambers?

NFPA 99, the U.S. health care fire code, groups hyperbaric chambers into three classes. Class A chambers hold multiple people and are multiplace chambers. Class B chambers hold a single person and are monoplace chambers. Class C chambers are for animals only. Inflatable soft-shell chambers sold for home use sit outside this medical-facility framework and are treated as a separate category.

Is a 1.3 ATA chamber real hyperbaric oxygen therapy?

Not by the medical definition. The UHMS defines HBOT as breathing near-100% oxygen at 1.4 ATA or higher, and recognized uses are commonly treated at 2.0 ATA or more. Mild 1.3 ATA chambers usually deliver room air and are FDA-cleared only for acute mountain sickness. They are not a substitute for medical HBOT for any listed condition.

What type of hyperbaric chamber is most common?

In outpatient clinics, the hard-shell monoplace chamber is the most common type. It holds one person at a time, usually in a clear acrylic tube filled with oxygen. Hospitals may also have multiplace chambers for several patients and critical care. Soft-shell chambers are common in homes and wellness studios, but they do not deliver medical-grade HBOT.

Can I tell a chamber's type from photos online?

Sometimes. A clear tube on a bed frame is usually a monoplace chamber, a steel room with seats is multiplace, and a fabric capsule with zippers is soft-shell. Photos do not show pressure, oxygen supply or supervision, so ask the provider directly what pressure they use, what you breathe, and what the chamber is FDA-cleared for.

Which type of chamber does your Modesto practice use?

We use hard-shell monoplace chambers. Each holds one patient breathing near-100% oxygen, with trained staff monitoring from outside the chamber.
