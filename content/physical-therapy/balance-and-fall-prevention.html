Safety First

When Dizziness or a Fall Is an Emergency

Call 911 if dizziness or imbalance comes on suddenly with any of these stroke warning signs: face drooping, arm or leg weakness, slurred speech, sudden vision loss or double vision, or a sudden severe headache. Also call 911 for chest pain or fainting.

After a fall, seek prompt care for a head injury (especially if you take blood thinners), hip or wrist pain that stops you bearing weight, or new confusion. See a doctor before starting therapy for new or unexplained dizziness, frequent near-fainting when you stand, or sudden hearing loss. Our physician can evaluate you and review medicines that may affect balance.

Understanding Fall Risk

Why Falls Happen as We Age

According to the CDC, about one in four adults 65 and older reports falling each year, and falls are the leading cause of injury in this age group. Many people who fall don't tell their doctor. Falling once also raises the chance of falling again, and fear of falling can lead people to move less, which weakens muscles and balance further.

Falls usually have several causes at once. Common risk factors include:

  • Leg weakness and trouble getting up from a chair
  • Balance and walking problems
  • Medicines that cause drowsiness or dizziness, such as some sleep aids, blood pressure medicines and pain medicines
  • Vision problems
  • Foot pain, poor footwear or numbness in the feet, often from peripheral neuropathy
  • Blood pressure that drops when you stand up
  • Home hazards such as loose rugs, clutter, poor lighting and stairs without rails

The CDC's STEADI program encourages clinicians to screen older adults for fall risk and address these factors one by one. Many can be changed.

Warning signs you may be at risk

  • You have fallen in the past year
  • You feel unsteady when standing or walking
  • You worry about falling
  • You hold onto furniture or walls when moving around your home

Your Plan

What Balance and Fall Prevention Therapy Involves

The assessment

Your physical therapist asks about past falls and near-falls, your home, your medicines and your goals. They use standard tests, such as timing how long it takes you to stand up from a chair, walk and sit back down, checking how long you can stand with your feet in different positions, and watching how you walk and turn. These tests help measure risk and track progress.

Treatment

  • Strength training for the legs and hips, which is central to preventing falls
  • Balance exercises that progress safely, from standing with support to walking, turning and stepping over objects
  • Walking practice, including choosing and using a cane or walker correctly if you need one
  • Getting up from the floor, so you know what to do if you do fall
  • A home program you can do safely, near a counter or sturdy chair

Research reviews, including a large Cochrane review, have found that exercise programs combining balance and functional training reduce the rate of falls in older adults living in the community. Benefits depend on doing the exercises regularly over time. Tai chi is another well-studied option you can continue in the community.

Dizziness

Vertigo and Vestibular Therapy

Your vestibular system, in the inner ear, helps control balance and keeps your vision steady when your head moves. When it isn't working well, you may feel spinning (vertigo), unsteadiness or motion sensitivity.

BPPV: the most common cause of vertigo

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) causes brief spinning, often when rolling over in bed, lying down or looking up. It happens when tiny crystals in the inner ear move out of place. It is often treated with repositioning maneuvers, such as the Epley maneuver, which a trained clinician performs after confirming the diagnosis with positional testing.

Vestibular rehabilitation

For other inner-ear balance problems, vestibular rehabilitation uses specific exercises for eye and head movement, balance and gradual exposure to motion. A clinical practice guideline from the Academy of Neurologic Physical Therapy supports vestibular rehabilitation for people with peripheral vestibular hypofunction.

Not all dizziness comes from the inner ear. Blood pressure, heart rhythm, medicines and other conditions can be involved, which is why our physician may evaluate you first.

Practical Steps

Home Safety Checklist

  • Clear the floor. Remove loose rugs, cords and clutter from walkways.
  • Light the way. Add night lights between the bedroom and bathroom.
  • Add support. Grab bars by the toilet and in the shower, and rails on both sides of stairs.
  • Wear safe shoes. Closed-back, non-slip shoes, even at home, instead of loose slippers or socks.
  • Review your medicines with a physician or pharmacist at least once a year.
  • Check your eyes yearly, and stand up slowly after sitting or lying down.

If daily tasks like bathing and dressing feel risky, occupational therapy can help with home setup and safer ways to do them.

Questions

Balance and Fall Prevention: Frequently Asked Questions

Can balance be improved in older adults?

Yes. Strength and balance respond to regular training at any age, and exercise programs have been shown to reduce falls in older adults.

What is vestibular therapy?

It is an exercise-based program for dizziness and imbalance caused by inner-ear problems, using eye, head and balance exercises.

Can physical therapy help vertigo?

Often, yes, especially BPPV, which is commonly treated with repositioning maneuvers. Other causes of dizziness need a medical evaluation.

Should I use a cane or walker?

It depends on your balance and walking. A physical therapist can tell you whether you need one, pick the right type and set it to the right height.
