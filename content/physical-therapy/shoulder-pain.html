Safety First

Shoulder Pain That Needs a Doctor First

Call 911 if left shoulder or arm pain comes with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating or nausea. Shoulder pain can be a sign of a heart attack.

Get prompt medical care for:

  • A shoulder that looks out of place, or you cannot lift your arm at all after a fall or injury
  • A hot, red, swollen joint or fever
  • Sudden weakness, numbness or tingling spreading down the arm

Our physician can evaluate you before therapy if needed.

The Most Common Cause

Physical Therapy for Rotator Cuff Problems

The rotator cuff is a group of four muscles and their tendons that keep the ball of the shoulder centered in its socket while you move. Rotator cuff problems range from irritated tendons (tendinopathy) to partial or full tears. Typical signs are an ache on the outside of the upper arm, pain when reaching overhead or behind your back, and pain lying on that side at night.

Many rotator cuff problems, including many partial tears and some full-thickness tears in older adults, improve with exercise-based care. The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons notes that non-surgical treatment relieves pain and improves function in many people with rotator cuff tears. Surgery is more likely to be considered after a sudden tear from injury, especially in younger, active people, or when weakness is significant.

What rotator cuff PT usually includes

  • Calming irritation by adjusting overhead lifting and sleeping positions for a while
  • Progressive strengthening of the rotator cuff and shoulder blade muscles
  • Restoring motion, especially reaching up and behind the back
  • Practicing the real tasks you need, such as lifting boxes onto a shelf, swimming or throwing

Tendons adapt slowly. Many people need several weeks of consistent loading before they notice clear change, and exact timelines vary from person to person.

Adhesive Capsulitis

Physical Therapy for Frozen Shoulder

Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis) happens when the capsule around the shoulder joint becomes thickened and tight. It causes pain and a steady loss of motion in every direction. It is more common between ages 40 and 60, in women, and in people with diabetes or thyroid conditions, or after a period of keeping the arm still.

Frozen shoulder usually moves through stages: a painful "freezing" stage, a stiff "frozen" stage, and a gradual "thawing" stage. The whole course often lasts many months and can take a year or more.

Physical therapy aims to keep and gradually regain motion without stirring up pain. In the painful stage, gentle stretching within comfort is usually best; more vigorous stretching comes later. Your therapist also helps you adapt daily tasks such as dressing and reaching. A physician may discuss other options alongside therapy, such as medicines. If you have diabetes, good blood sugar control is part of overall care.

Other Causes

Other Common Causes of Shoulder Pain

  • Subacromial pain ("impingement"). Pain lifting the arm out to the side or overhead. Exercise therapy is the recommended first treatment for most people.
  • Shoulder instability. A feeling that the shoulder slips or gives way, sometimes after a dislocation. Strengthening and control training are central.
  • Shoulder arthritis. Stiffness and grinding, more common with age. PT helps maintain motion and strength.
  • Pain referred from the neck. Pain that starts in the neck and spreads to the shoulder blade or arm. See neck pain physical therapy.

Your therapist's exam helps tell these apart. If symptoms don't fit a shoulder problem, or don't improve as expected, they will involve our physician.

Your Plan

What Shoulder Physical Therapy Involves

The evaluation

Your therapist asks how the pain began, which movements hurt, and how it affects sleep, work and hobbies. They measure how far your arm moves on its own and with help, test strength in different directions, look at how your shoulder blade moves, and screen your neck.

Treatment

  • Exercise that progresses from gentle motion to resistance bands to weights
  • Hands-on techniques for stiff joints and tight tissue, when helpful
  • Posture and work setup tips for desk work, driving and overhead jobs
  • Sleep strategies, such as hugging a pillow when lying on the other side
  • A home program with clear guidance on how much is enough

What you can do now

Keep the arm moving gently within comfort; long periods of not moving the shoulder can contribute to stiffness. Avoid repeated heavy overhead lifting for now. Ask a clinician before starting new pain medicines, especially if you have kidney, stomach or heart problems.

If you've had shoulder surgery, rehab follows your surgeon's protocol; read about post-surgical rehab. If daily tasks like dressing and grooming are the main struggle, occupational therapy may also help.

Questions

Shoulder Pain Physical Therapy: Frequently Asked Questions

Can a torn rotator cuff heal with physical therapy?

A full tear does not knit back together on its own, but many people regain good, comfortable function with exercise because surrounding muscles compensate. Some tears need a surgeon's opinion.

How long does frozen shoulder last?

Often many months and sometimes a year or more. Physical therapy aims to protect and regain motion and help you manage daily life during that time.

Why does my shoulder hurt more at night?

Lying on the sore side compresses irritated tissue, and fewer distractions make pain more noticeable. Pillow support and position changes often help.

Should I rest my shoulder completely?

Usually not. Avoid what clearly aggravates it, but keep gentle movement going to prevent stiffness, unless a doctor has told you to immobilize it.

Do I need an MRI before shoulder physical therapy?

Usually not at first. Rotator cuff changes are common on imaging even in people without pain, so scans are generally reserved for significant injuries, marked weakness or pain that doesn't improve with a fair trial of therapy. A clinician decides whether you need one.

Can I keep working with shoulder pain?

Often yes, with changes such as keeping heavy loads close to your body and limiting repeated overhead reaching for a while. Your therapist can suggest adjustments for your specific job.
