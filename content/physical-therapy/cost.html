Behind the Price

What Drives the Cost of Physical Therapy

There is no single price for physical therapy. Prices differ from clinic to clinic and region to region, and online averages often mix together insurance rates, hospital charges and cash prices. Rather than rely on a national figure that may not match Modesto, it helps to understand what moves the price.

Type of visit

The first visit is an evaluation. It usually costs more than follow-up visits because it takes longer and includes testing and a written plan of care. Follow-up treatment visits are often priced per visit.

Where you're treated

Hospital-based outpatient departments may add facility fees that private clinics do not. Independent clinics and physician practices often have simpler pricing for people paying directly.

Visit length and what's included

Some clinics price by the visit, others by time or by the services delivered. Ask whether one-on-one time with a licensed physical therapist is included, and whether exercise equipment, taping or a printed home program cost extra.

How many visits you need

This is often the biggest factor. A simple problem may need a few visits to learn a self-management plan. Rehab after a joint replacement or ACL surgery can take many visits over months.

Course Math

How to Estimate the Total Cost of a Plan of Care

Comparing clinics by one visit's price can mislead you. A fairer comparison is the expected total:

Evaluation price + (follow-up price × expected number of visits) + any extras = estimated total.

Your therapist can usually estimate a visit range after the evaluation, and that range may change as you progress. Remember that most of the work happens at home: people who do their home program consistently often need fewer visits to reach their goals.

California's 12-visit checkpoint

Under California law, as of 2026, you can start physical therapy without a referral, but after 45 calendar days or 12 visits, whichever comes first, a physician must examine you and sign your plan of care to continue. If you're budgeting, plan for that visit too. At a physician-led practice, that review happens in the same place. Rules can change; read more in our guide to physical therapy without a referral.

Be an Informed Patient

Questions to Ask Any Clinic About Cost

  1. What is the price of the first evaluation, and of each follow-up visit?
  2. Is the price per visit or per unit of time? How long is a typical visit?
  3. Will I be treated by a licensed physical therapist for the whole visit?
  4. Are there extra charges for supplies, equipment or reports?
  5. How many visits do you expect I'll need, and when will you review that?
  6. What happens at the 45-day or 12-visit point, and is a physician visit an extra cost?
  7. Can I get a written estimate before I begin?

Your right to a good faith estimate

Under the federal No Surprises Act, if you are uninsured or not using insurance, health care providers and facilities generally must give you a good faith estimate of expected charges when you schedule care or when you ask. If a final bill is substantially higher than the estimate, you may be able to dispute it. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services explains the rules on its website.

Practical Tips

Ways to Keep Physical Therapy Costs Down

  • Start early. Problems caught early often need fewer visits than pain that has lasted months.
  • Do your home program. It is the most effective way to get more out of each paid visit.
  • Ask about spacing visits. Once you know your exercises, visits can often be spread out.
  • Use HSA or FSA funds. Physical therapy for a medical condition is generally an eligible medical expense under IRS rules. Confirm with your plan administrator.
  • Check your coverage. If you have a health plan, ask about your deductible, copay and visit limits for PT; sometimes paying directly and using coverage compare differently than you expect. Our insurance guide explains coverage in general.

Questions

Physical Therapy Cost: Frequently Asked Questions

How much is physical therapy without insurance?

It varies by clinic, setting and the number of visits you need. Ask for the evaluation price, the follow-up price and an expected visit range, then estimate the total. Call us for our current pricing.

Why does the first PT visit cost more?

The evaluation takes longer and includes testing, a diagnosis of the movement problem and a written plan of care.

Can I use my HSA or FSA for physical therapy?

Generally yes, when it treats a medical condition. Check with your plan administrator before you start.

How many physical therapy visits will I need?

It depends on your condition, goals and home exercise. Your therapist gives an estimate after the evaluation and reviews it as you progress.

Can I get a cost estimate before I start?

Yes. If you are uninsured or paying directly, federal rules generally entitle you to a good faith estimate, and we review costs with you before treatment.
