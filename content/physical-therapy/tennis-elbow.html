Outer Elbow Pain

What Is Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis)?

Tennis elbow, or lateral epicondylitis, is irritation of the tendon that attaches the wrist-extending muscles of the forearm to the bony bump on the outside of the elbow. Many clinicians now call it lateral elbow tendinopathy, because it involves tendon changes from overload more than ongoing inflammation.

It usually builds up from repeated gripping and wrist use, such as using tools, typing, lifting with palms down, pruning, or racquet sports. It is most common between ages 35 and 55. Typical signs include:

  • Pain on the outside of the elbow that may spread down the forearm
  • Pain gripping a cup, shaking hands, turning a doorknob or lifting a bag
  • Weaker grip than usual

Inner Elbow Pain

Golfer's Elbow (Medial Epicondylitis)

Golfer's elbow is the same kind of problem on the inside of the elbow, where the wrist-bending muscles attach. It causes pain on the inner elbow with gripping, throwing or lifting with palms up. It is treated in a similar way, with exercises aimed at the inner forearm muscles. Tingling in the ring and little fingers along with inner elbow pain may point to an irritated ulnar nerve instead, which your clinician should check.

Safety First

Elbow Symptoms That Need a Doctor First

See a physician first, or get urgent care, if you have:

  • Elbow pain after a fall, with swelling, deformity, or trouble bending or straightening the arm
  • A hot, red, swollen elbow, especially with fever
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness in the hand that is getting worse
  • A sudden pop at the front of the elbow while lifting, followed by bruising, which can be a biceps tendon tear

Your Plan

What Tennis Elbow Physical Therapy Involves

Progressive loading

The core of treatment is exercise that gradually loads the tendon so it can adapt. Your therapist starts at a level your elbow tolerates and increases it over weeks. Common lateral epicondylitis exercises include:

  • Isometric wrist extension, pressing the back of your hand up against resistance without moving, often used early when pain is high
  • Slow wrist extension with a light weight, focusing on lowering slowly
  • Grip strengthening with a soft ball or putty
  • Forearm rotation with a light hammer or bar
  • Shoulder and upper back strengthening, so the whole arm shares the work

Some discomfort during these exercises is expected. A common guide is that pain should stay mild and settle by the next day.

Activity modification

You don't have to stop using your arm. Your therapist helps you find which tasks overload the tendon and how to change them for now: lifting with palms up, using two hands, choosing tools with thicker handles, adjusting your keyboard and mouse, or taking short breaks during repetitive work.

Counterforce brace

A counterforce brace is a strap worn just below the elbow. It may ease pain during activity for some people by spreading out the pull on the tendon. It is a short-term helper, not a stand-alone treatment.

Hands-on care

Manual techniques for the elbow, wrist or neck may be added to exercise when they help.

Evidence

What About Injections?

Corticosteroid injections are sometimes used for tennis elbow. Research shows they can relieve pain in the short term, but results at a year are often no better, or worse, than exercise or waiting, and recurrence is more common. Exercise-based care has strong support for longer-term results.

We do not offer injections at our practice. If you and your physician decide an injection or other procedure is worth considering, we can refer you to a specialist. Most tennis elbow improves without surgery, though it can take several months. If hand and wrist symptoms are part of the picture, our hand therapy team can help too.

Questions

Tennis Elbow Physical Therapy: Frequently Asked Questions

How long does tennis elbow take to heal?

Tennis elbow often improves slowly. Many people feel better over several weeks to a few months of steady exercise and activity changes, and some take longer. Most people recover without surgery. Your physical therapist tracks your grip strength and pain and adjusts the plan, but timelines vary and can't be promised.

What are the best exercises for lateral epicondylitis?

Common choices are isometric wrist extension holds, slow wrist extension with a light weight, grip strengthening and forearm rotation with a light bar. Shoulder and upper back strength also help. The right starting level depends on how sore your elbow is, so a physical therapist can set and progress a program for you.

Does a tennis elbow brace work?

A counterforce brace, worn just below the elbow, may reduce pain during gripping and lifting for some people. It works best as a short-term helper while you build tendon strength through exercise. It should feel snug but not cut off circulation. Your therapist can show you where to place it and when to wear it.

Should I get a cortisone shot for tennis elbow?

That is a decision for you and your physician. Research shows steroid injections can ease pain for a few weeks, but longer-term results are often no better than exercise and recurrence is more common. We do not offer injections here. We can refer you to a specialist if you decide to consider one.

What is the difference between tennis elbow and golfer's elbow?

Both are tendon overload problems. Tennis elbow causes pain on the outside of the elbow, where the wrist-extending muscles attach. Golfer's elbow causes pain on the inside, where the wrist-bending muscles attach. Treatment for both centers on progressive loading exercises and activity changes, aimed at the muscles involved.
