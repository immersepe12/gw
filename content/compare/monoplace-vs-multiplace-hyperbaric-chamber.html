At a Glance

Monoplace and Multiplace Chambers Side by Side

A monoplace chamber holds one person. A multiplace chamber is a room-sized vessel that holds two or more people at once, often with a trained attendant inside. Both are hard-shell pressure vessels, and both can deliver true hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) at the pressures recognized medical uses call for. The main difference is how the oxygen reaches you and who is in there with you.

Oxygen vs. Air

How Monoplace and Multiplace Chambers Are Pressurized

In a typical monoplace chamber, the whole tube fills with near-100% oxygen and is pressurized. You simply breathe normally. There is nothing to strap to your face, which many people find more comfortable.

A multiplace chamber is pressurized with ordinary compressed air, because filling a room with pure oxygen for several people would be a serious fire risk. Each person breathes oxygen through a clear plastic hood that seals around the neck, or a snug mask. Exhaled gas is piped out of the chamber so the room's oxygen level stays close to normal.

Both approaches can reach the treatment pressures used for recognized indications, commonly 2.0 to 3.0 atmospheres absolute (ATA). The pressure for your treatment depends on the condition and the protocol your physician follows, not on the chamber type alone. For a plain-language tour of the terms, see our hyperbaric glossary.

Inside the Chamber

What a Session Feels Like in Each Chamber

Monoplace sessions

You lie on a padded bed that slides into the clear tube. You can see out the whole time, talk with the operator over an intercom, and often watch a screen outside the chamber. You are alone inside, but a trained staff member watches you throughout. Because the tube is clear, many people who worry about tight spaces cope better than they expect.

Multiplace sessions

You sit or lie in a larger room with other patients. An inside attendant can help you clear your ears, adjust your hood or respond quickly if you feel unwell. That hands-on access is one reason hospitals use multiplace chambers for very ill patients, such as people on ventilators or those needing close monitoring.

Air breaks

Many protocols include short "air breaks," a few minutes of breathing normal air between oxygen periods, to lower the risk of oxygen toxicity. In a multiplace chamber you just lift off the hood or mask. In a monoplace chamber, air breaks are given through a mask supplied with air, where the chamber is set up for it. Your care team follows the protocol for your condition.

Claustrophobia

Some people feel more at ease in a roomy multiplace chamber with others around. Others prefer the privacy and clear view of a monoplace tube, and dislike wearing a hood. Neither is better for everyone. Read our tips in ear pressure and claustrophobia in HBOT.

Safety

Fire Safety Standards for Each Chamber Type

Oxygen makes things burn more easily, so chamber safety is taken seriously. In the United States, hyperbaric facilities follow NFPA 99, the Health Care Facilities Code. It sorts chambers by who is inside:

  • Class A: multiple occupants (multiplace chambers)
  • Class B: a single occupant (monoplace chambers)
  • Class C: animals only

The vessels themselves are built to the ASME PVHO-1 pressure vessel code. Whatever the chamber type, expect strict rules: no phones, lighters, hand warmers, oils or certain fabrics inside, and a cotton gown or approved clothing. Our guide to what to wear and bring to HBOT covers the details.

Cost Drivers

What Drives the Cost of Each Chamber Type

Multiplace chambers are large installations with their own air systems, staff teams and building requirements. They are expensive to build and run, so they are found mainly at hospitals and big regional centers. Monoplace chambers cost less to install and operate, which is why most outpatient HBOT centers use them.

What you pay depends far more on the setting, the condition and your coverage than on the chamber type. Hospital-based programs and independent clinics bill differently; see hospital vs. independent HBOT centers and our HBOT cost guide.

Choosing

Which Chamber Type Suits Whom

  • Critically ill or emergency patients, such as severe carbon monoxide poisoning or decompression sickness needing long tables, are often treated in hospital multiplace chambers where an attendant can provide hands-on care.
  • Stable outpatients with planned courses, such as certain diabetic foot ulcers or late radiation injury, are commonly treated in monoplace chambers.
  • People who cannot tolerate a hood or mask may find monoplace sessions easier.
  • People who need frequent help during a session may do better in a multiplace setting.

Questions

Monoplace vs. Multiplace: Frequently Asked Questions

Is a monoplace or multiplace chamber better?

Neither is better across the board. Both are hard-shell chambers that can deliver true HBOT at medical pressures. Multiplace chambers suit critically ill patients who need an attendant inside. Monoplace chambers suit stable outpatients and people who dislike wearing a hood. The right choice depends on your condition, your health and the setting, which your physician helps you weigh.

Do you breathe pure oxygen in a monoplace chamber?

Usually yes. A typical monoplace chamber is filled and pressurized with near-100% oxygen, so you breathe it directly from the chamber air without a mask. In a multiplace chamber the room holds compressed air, and each person breathes oxygen through a hood or mask. Either way, the oxygen level and pressure follow a protocol your physician selects.

Can someone stay with me in the chamber?

In a multiplace chamber, a trained attendant often sits inside with patients. In a monoplace chamber you are the only person inside, but a trained operator watches you through the clear wall the whole time and can talk with you by intercom. If you are anxious about being alone, mention it during your assessment so the team can plan for it.

What chamber does Generations Wellness use?

We use hard-shell monoplace chambers at our Modesto practice. Each holds one patient, with staff monitoring from outside. We provide HBOT only for FDA-cleared indications and only after a physician assessment. If your situation calls for hospital-level or emergency care, such as a multiplace program with an inside attendant, we will tell you and help you find the right setting.

Are multiplace chambers safer than monoplace chambers?

Both types are safe when built to the ASME PVHO-1 code, operated under NFPA 99 and run by trained staff. Multiplace chambers lower fire risk by holding compressed air rather than pure oxygen, while monoplace chambers use strict rules on what enters the chamber. Side effects such as ear pressure are possible in either type and are reviewed with you beforehand.
