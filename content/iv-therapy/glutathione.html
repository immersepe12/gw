The Basics

What Glutathione Is

Glutathione is a small molecule your body builds from three amino acids: cysteine, glutamic acid and glycine. It is found in nearly every cell and is especially concentrated in the liver. It works as an antioxidant, which means it helps the body handle reactive molecules that form during normal metabolism. Your body also uses it in many routine chemical reactions.

Because the body makes its own glutathione, you do not need to get it from food the way you need vitamins. Fruits, vegetables and meats contain some, and your body uses protein from your diet to make more.

How glutathione is taken

People take glutathione by mouth, as an inhaled form, or by IV. Taken by mouth, much of it is broken down during digestion, which is one reason some clinics offer it by IV. Honest Answers

What the Research Shows About IV Glutathione

MedlinePlus, the National Library of Medicine's consumer health site, rates the evidence for glutathione as insufficient for most of the uses people take it for. Researchers have studied IV glutathione in specific settings, such as Parkinson's disease and certain chemotherapy side effects, but these studies were small, results were mixed, and none has made it a standard treatment.

For healthy people, there is little good-quality evidence that adding glutathione by IV changes how you feel or improves any health outcome. That is the honest summary, and we think you deserve to hear it before you decide.

Why we make no skin-lightening or "glow" claims

Glutathione is widely marketed online for lighter or brighter skin. We do not offer it for that purpose and we do not make that claim. The evidence for skin effects is weak, there are no FDA-approved injectable glutathione products for skin lightening, and regulators in the U.S. and other countries have warned about safety problems with injectable skin-lightening products. The FTC also requires that health claims be backed by reliable scientific evidence.

Why oral and IV studies don't transfer

You may see glutathione articles that cite studies of pills, creams or inhaled forms. Results from one form do not tell you what happens with another, because the dose, route and how the body handles it are different. The FTC's guidance makes the same point for marketers: evidence has to match the actual product, dose and route. A study of a glutathione cream says nothing reliable about a glutathione IV.

Can you raise your own glutathione levels?

Your body regulates glutathione on its own. Eating enough protein, along with fruits and vegetables, gives it the building blocks it needs. Researchers are still working out whether, and how, supplements change glutathione levels inside cells in a way that matters for health. For now, there is no blood test we use to decide that someone "needs" glutathione.

Other claims we don't make

  • That glutathione cleanses or flushes toxins from your body
  • That it slows aging or treats any disease
  • That it strengthens your immune system
  • That it speeds recovery from exercise, illness or drinking

Safety

Side Effects and Who Should Avoid It

Glutathione at the amounts in our blend is generally well tolerated by screened adults, but nothing given by IV is free of risk. Possible side effects include:

  • Bruising, soreness or swelling where the IV was placed
  • A flushed or warm feeling, stomach upset or a change in taste
  • Lightheadedness
  • Rarely, vein irritation, infection or an allergic reaction, including rash or trouble breathing

People with asthma should mention it at screening, because inhaled glutathione has been linked to breathing problems in some people with asthma. We also take extra care, or do not give an IV, for people who are pregnant or breastfeeding (safety is not well studied), have heart, kidney or liver disease, have had reactions to IV ingredients, or take medicines that may interact. Some cancer treatments may interact with antioxidants, so if you are being treated for cancer, talk with your oncologist first.

Glutathione IV: Frequently Asked Questions

What does glutathione do in the body?

It is an antioxidant your body makes. It helps cells handle reactive by-products of normal metabolism and takes part in many routine chemical reactions.

Will glutathione lighten my skin?

We do not offer glutathione for skin lightening and make no skin claims. Evidence is weak, and injectable skin-lightening products have raised safety concerns.

Can I get a glutathione-only drip?

Is IV glutathione safe?
