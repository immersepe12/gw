What's Inside

The Four Ingredients in the Energy Drip

Each Energy Drip is given through a small IV line in your arm, mixed in IV fluid. Below, each ingredient is described the way the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and MedlinePlus describe it. These are descriptions of what the nutrients are, not promises of what a drip will do for you.

Vitamin B12

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is a vitamin your body needs to make red blood cells and DNA and to keep nerve cells healthy. It plays a role in normal energy metabolism. It is found naturally in meat, fish, eggs and dairy. People who eat no animal foods, older adults, and people with certain stomach or bowel conditions are more likely to run low. Read more in our vitamin B12 guide.

B-complex

"B-complex" means a mix of B vitamins. The B family includes thiamin (B1), riboflavin (B2), niacin (B3), pantothenic acid (B5), B6, biotin (B7), folate (B9) and B12. They help your body turn the food you eat into usable energy. They are water-soluble, so the body does not store most of them in large amounts and passes extra in urine. This is why urine can look bright yellow after B vitamins.

Taurine

Taurine is an amino acid-like compound (an amino sulfonic acid) that your body makes on its own. It is found in high amounts in the heart, brain, eyes and muscles, and in foods like meat and seafood. Taurine is a common energy drink ingredient. Research on extra taurine in humans is still early, and there is no established benefit from taking it by IV in healthy people.

Glutathione

Glutathione is an antioxidant your body makes from three amino acids. It is found in nearly every cell, where it helps handle normal by-products of metabolism. MedlinePlus notes there is not enough reliable evidence to rate glutathione for most uses people take it for. See our glutathione guide for details.

What the Evidence Shows, and What It Doesn't

We want you to make this choice with clear eyes. Here is a plain summary:

IngredientEstablished medical useEvidence for people without a deficiency

Put simply: evidence that IV vitamins help people who are not deficient is limited. Some people tell us they enjoy the visit and the time to rest. That is a fair reason to choose it, as long as you know what the research says. Individual experiences vary.

If you are often tired, please do not rely on a drip. Ongoing fatigue can be a sign of anemia, thyroid disease, sleep apnea, depression or other conditions. Our physicians can evaluate the cause, and blood tests can check your B12 level.

Comparisons

Energy Drip vs. the Myers' Cocktail

The "Myers' cocktail" is a well-known IV blend named after a Baltimore physician. It usually contains magnesium, calcium, B vitamins and vitamin C. A 2002 review by Alan Gaby described its use, but the studies behind it were small, and larger controlled trials have not shown clear benefits.

The Energy Drip is a different blend. It shares the B vitamins but adds taurine and glutathione and does not include the magnesium, calcium or vitamin C of a classic Myers'. We do not offer a Myers' cocktail, and we do not claim the Energy Drip works better or worse than one.

At Our Practice

What an Energy Drip Visit Looks Like

  1. Screening. A licensed clinician reviews your health history, medications and vital signs. A physician decides whether the IV is appropriate and orders it.
  2. The drip. A small catheter is placed in a vein. A drip commonly takes about 30 to 60 minutes. You can sit, read or rest.
  3. After. The catheter is removed and a small bandage applied. Most people go on with their day.

Possible side effects include bruising or soreness at the site, a warm or flushed feeling, a metallic taste, lightheadedness and, rarely, vein irritation, infection or allergic reaction. People with heart, kidney or liver disease, or who are pregnant, may not be good candidates. See the full IV safety guide.

Questions

Energy Drip: Frequently Asked Questions

Will the Energy Drip give me more energy?

We can't promise that. The B vitamins play a role in normal energy metabolism, but evidence that IV vitamins raise energy in people without a deficiency is limited. Vitamin B12 has a proven use for treating a documented deficiency, but there is no proven benefit for energy if your levels are normal. A physician screening comes before any drip.

Does the Energy Drip contain caffeine?

Its listed ingredients are vitamin B12, B-complex, taurine and glutathione in IV fluid. Ask your clinician to review the full ingredient list with you at screening. Each drip is given through a small IV line in your arm. These are descriptions of what the nutrients are, not promises of what a drip will do for you.

How often can I get one?

That is a physician decision based on your health and screening. There is no evidence that frequent drips add benefit for people with normal vitamin levels. B vitamins are water-soluble, so the body passes extra in urine instead of storing it. Most healthy people can also stay hydrated by drinking.

Is a B12 shot better than a drip?

For a documented B12 deficiency, injections into the muscle or high-dose oral B12 are the standard treatments. Our B12 guide explains the differences. People who eat no animal foods, older adults, and people with certain stomach or bowel conditions are more likely to run low. A clinician can check your levels before suggesting any treatment.

How long does an IV drip take?

An IV drip commonly takes about 30 to 60 minutes, plus time beforehand for a health screening and vital signs. Your first visit may take longer because a clinician reviews your history and decides whether IV therapy is appropriate for you. You can usually sit, read or rest during the infusion. Tell staff right away if you feel unwell at any point.
