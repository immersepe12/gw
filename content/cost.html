Course Math

What a Full Course Costs: 20 vs. 40 Sessions

HBOT is rarely a single visit. For the conditions HBOT is cleared for, a physician commonly recommends 20 to 40 sessions, usually one a day, five days a week. That makes the course total the number that really matters. The table below simply multiplies session count by three common per-session prices.

Illustrative course totals: session count multiplied by per-session price. Not a quote.
SessionsAt $150 per sessionAt $250 per sessionAt $350 per session
20 sessions$3,000$5,000$7,000
30 sessions$4,500$7,500$10,500
40 sessions$6,000$10,000$14,000

Published national guides quote roughly $5,000 to $9,000 for a 20-session course and $10,000 to $18,000 for 40 sessions. Your physician sets the session count and reviews your progress along the way, so the course can be adjusted as your tissue responds. That's also why no one should prepay for 40 sessions before a physician has assessed whether HBOT is appropriate.

Behind the Numbers

Why HBOT Prices Differ So Much

Chamber type and pressure

The Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical Society (UHMS) defines HBOT as breathing near-100% oxygen inside a chamber pressurized to at least 1.4 times normal atmospheric pressure (1.4 ATA). Every UHMS-recognized indication calls for 2.0 ATA or more. Soft-sided "mild" chambers typically run at about 1.3 ATA on regular air, which is why they cost less, and why they are not interchangeable with medical HBOT. See how hard-shell and soft-shell chambers differ.

Hospital vs. independent center

Hospital outpatient departments carry facility overhead that independent centers don't. Published cost guides estimate that uninsured patients often pay 30–60% less at an independent center for a comparable session. For a fuller comparison, read hospital vs. independent HBOT centers.

Physician supervision and staffing

Medical HBOT is supervised. In billing terms, physician attendance is its own service (CPT code 99183), separate from the facility charge for chamber time (HCPCS G0277, billed per 30-minute interval). That supervision, along with trained staff monitoring every session, is part of what you are paying for.

Session length and course length

A typical session runs about 90 to 120 minutes including pressurization and depressurization. Longer sessions and longer courses cost more in total, which is why the course total, not the per-session price, is the fairer comparison between centers.

Paying for Care

Self-Pay and Insurance: Which Applies to You

Generations Wellness currently offers HBOT on a self-pay basis while we work toward insurance contracts. That means we do not bill insurance or Medicare today, and we will tell you so plainly before you begin.

It is still worth knowing how coverage works in general. Insurance, including Medicare, can cover HBOT for certain FDA-cleared indications when specific medical criteria are met. Medicare's rules are set out in National Coverage Determination 20.29. For a selected diabetic foot ulcer, for example, the wound must be Wagner grade 3 or higher and must have shown no measurable healing after at least 30 days of standard wound care. Uses outside the covered list are self-pay everywhere.

At facilities that do bill Medicare, patients typically pay the Part B deductible ($283 in 2026) and then 20% coinsurance. Published estimates put that at roughly $90 to $115 per session. That is a general range, not a quote.

If you think you may have a covered indication, tell us when you call. We will explain your options honestly, including when another setting may make more financial sense for you. For the details, read whether insurance may cover HBOT and Medicare's HBOT coverage criteria.

Questions

HBOT Cost: Frequently Asked Questions

How much does hyperbaric oxygen therapy cost per session?

Nationally, medical-grade HBOT commonly runs $150 to $450 per session at independent centers and $250 to $450 in hospital outpatient departments. Spa-style soft chambers cost less because they are not medical HBOT. We review your pricing clearly at your consultation.

How much does a full course cost without insurance?

Published national guides quote about $5,000 to $9,000 for 20 sessions and $10,000 to $18,000 for 40, depending on setting. Your physician sets the session count, so your total depends on your plan.

Do you accept insurance or Medicare?

Not yet. We currently offer HBOT on a self-pay basis while we work toward insurance contracts. Insurance and Medicare can cover HBOT for certain FDA-cleared indications when criteria are met, so if that may apply to you, ask us and we'll explain your options honestly.

Can I use my HSA or FSA for HBOT?

Generally yes, when HBOT is prescribed by a physician for a medical condition. Confirm eligibility with your plan administrator before you start.

Why are some HBOT centers so much cheaper?

Lower prices often reflect soft-sided chambers running at about 1.3 ATA on air, which do not meet the medical definition of HBOT. Hospital facility fees push prices the other way. Compare chamber type and physician supervision, not just the price.
