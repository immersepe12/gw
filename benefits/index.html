Benefits & Candidacy

Benefits of Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy: Who It May Help

You'll notice careful words on this page — "supports," "may help," "can support." That's deliberate. HBOT at Generations Wellness is used as part of a physician-directed care plan, and we describe its potential the way the evidence does: honestly.

The Foundation

How HBOT May Support the Body's Healing Response

Healing tissue needs oxygen — and the tissues that struggle most to heal are usually the ones oxygen struggles to reach. In a pressurized chamber, extra oxygen dissolves directly into your blood plasma, helping it reach compromised areas, supporting the formation of new blood vessels, and helping reduce swelling naturally.

The full mechanism, session experience, and safety protocols are covered on our main therapy page: see how a session works.

Potential Benefits

Potential Benefits of HBOT

Six ways increased oxygen availability may support your body — each one qualified, because that's how honest medicine talks.

  • Supports Wound Healing and Tissue Recovery

    More available oxygen supports the cellular work of wound repair — which is why HBOT is commonly used alongside wound care.

  • Helps Improve Oxygen Delivery to Compromised Tissues

    Oxygen dissolved in plasma can reach areas where red blood cells pass with difficulty — the tissues that need help most.

  • May Assist Healing After Radiation-Related Tissue Injury

    Radiation can leave tissue with a diminished blood supply long after treatment ends. HBOT may assist that tissue's slow rebuilding work.

  • Can Support Recovery in Selected Diabetic Wounds

    For selected diabetic wounds and chronic non-healing wounds, HBOT can support recovery — always as determined by a physician.

  • May Help Reduce Swelling and Support Circulation

    Increased oxygen availability may help reduce swelling naturally and support the circulation healing depends on.

  • Supports the Body's Natural Healing Response

    Ultimately, HBOT doesn't heal anything by itself — it supports the healing systems your body already runs, by feeding them oxygen.

Candidacy

Who May Benefit From HBOT?

HBOT is most often considered — under physician direction — for people managing difficult healing situations like these:

  • Chronic and non-healing wounds that have stalled despite good wound care.
  • Radiation-related tissue injury — delayed soft-tissue or bone injury after radiation therapy.
  • Selected diabetic foot wounds, where oxygen supply is a limiting factor in recovery.
  • Compromised skin grafts or flaps that need extra support to take hold.
  • Other difficult healing conditions — as determined by a physician after assessment.

The Honest List

FDA-Cleared Uses of Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy

The FDA has cleared hyperbaric chambers for a specific set of conditions. Here are notable examples:

  • Select non-healing diabetic wounds
  • Delayed radiation injury (soft tissue & bone)
  • Compromised skin grafts and flaps
  • Certain serious infections
  • Crush injury
  • Carbon monoxide poisoning
  • Decompression sickness
  • Air or gas embolism
  • Severe anemia
  • Severe thermal burns
  • Refractory osteomyelitis
  • Sudden sensorineural hearing loss

Just as important is what's not on this list. HBOT has not been proven safe or effective for many conditions you may see marketed elsewhere — and we won't market it for them. Our physician will tell you honestly whether HBOT is appropriate for your situation. For source material, see the FDA's consumer update on HBOT and the UHMS indications list.

Clear Eyes

What HBOT Is Not

HBOT is not a cure-all, and it is not a substitute for medical care. It complements — never replaces — the treatment prescribed by your doctors. We promise no specific outcomes, because individual results genuinely vary. And whether HBOT is right for you isn't a marketing decision: it's a medical one, made by a physician after reviewing your history and condition.

If that candor sounds different from other things you've read about hyperbaric therapy — that's the point.

Important

A physician assessment is required before beginning HBOT. HBOT complements, never replaces, medical treatment.
