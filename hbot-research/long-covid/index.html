Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy for Long COVID: What the Research Shows

The evidence is mixed and early. One sham-controlled trial of 40 sessions reported better thinking skills and symptoms; a trial of 10 sessions found no difference from placebo. Long COVID is not an FDA-cleared indication for HBOT, and Generations Wellness does not offer HBOT for it.

Physician-directed care under Dr. Rajwinder Singh Bahia, MD · Updated September 17, 2026

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is not an FDA-cleared indication for long COVID, and Generations Wellness does not market or offer HBOT for that purpose. The research is genuinely interesting and genuinely unsettled. In 2022 an Israeli team reported that 40 sessions of HBOT improved attention, executive function and several symptoms compared with a sham chamber in 73 people with post-COVID cognitive complaints, and that gains persisted more than a year later in the people they could follow. In 2025 a Swedish trial of 10 sessions in 80 people found no difference from placebo on its main physical-function measures. This page lays out both, what they measured, what they did not, and how our physician-directed team talks about it.

Not an FDA-cleared indication

Hyperbaric chambers are FDA-cleared for a specific list of conditions. Long COVID is not on it, and it is not among the UHMS-recognized indications. Generations Wellness does not market or provide HBOT as a treatment for long COVID. This page summarizes the published research so you can read it for yourself. See the cleared conditions list.

Background

Why People Ask About HBOT for Long COVID

Long COVID, which the World Health Organization calls post COVID-19 condition, describes symptoms that usually begin within three months of a COVID-19 infection and last at least two months without another explanation. Common complaints include fatigue, "brain fog," trouble concentrating, poor sleep, shortness of breath and pain. For many people, these symptoms make work and family life hard, and there is still no established treatment that works for everyone. It is understandable that people look for options.

Researchers have proposed several reasons symptoms may persist. These include injury to the smallest blood vessels, ongoing inflammation in the brain and body, and problems with how cells produce energy. These are hypotheses, not settled facts.

Hyperbaric researchers have suggested that HBOT might play a role because, in other settings, high-pressure oxygen has been studied for its effects on new blood-vessel growth and inflammatory signaling. You can read more about angiogenesis and anti-inflammatory effects in our explainer on how HBOT works. Whether any of that translates into meaningful improvement for people with long COVID is exactly what the trials below tried to test.

Why two good trials can disagree

Both randomized trials were double-blind and used a comparison group, which makes them stronger than most HBOT research. So why did they reach different conclusions? The most obvious difference is dose. The Israeli trial used 40 sessions over about two months; the Swedish trial used 10. The trials also measured different things. The Israeli trial's main outcome was cognitive testing, while the Swedish trial's main outcome was physical functioning on a questionnaire called RAND-36. And the participants were not identical: the Israeli trial enrolled people with cognitive complaints specifically.

None of this tells us which result is "right." It tells us the question is still open. A larger trial using a longer course and patient-relevant outcomes would be needed to settle it.

Clinical trials

Trials of HBOT for post-COVID condition have been registered on ClinicalTrials.gov, including NCT04842448. If you are interested in HBOT for long COVID, a registered trial is the setting where it is delivered under research oversight, with the results feeding back into what everyone knows. Search ClinicalTrials.gov for "hyperbaric" and "post-COVID."

The Limits

What the Research Does Not Show

It is just as important to be clear about what these studies cannot tell you:

  • Not that HBOT cures long COVID. No study claims that, and the only large controlled trials disagree.
  • Not that results apply to everyone. Participants in the positive trial were mostly middle-aged adults with cognitive complaints lasting at least three months. People with other symptom patterns were not studied the same way.
  • Not that a short course works. The 10-session trial found no difference from placebo.
  • Not the right dose. No study has compared different numbers of sessions or pressures head to head.
  • Not long-term durability. The only follow-up beyond a year had no control group and followed fewer than half of the original participants.
  • Not safety in people with lung damage from COVID. Lung disease can be a relative contraindication for HBOT, and people with significant lung injury need careful individual assessment. Read about relative contraindications including lung disease.
  • Not insurance coverage. No insurer covers HBOT for long COVID. See why investigational uses are not covered.

HBOT also carries its own side effects, even when it is used for cleared indications. The most common are ear and sinus pressure, temporary nearsightedness and tiredness. Rare but serious risks include oxygen toxicity seizures and lung injury from pressure changes.

Reading the Numbers

How to Read "Significant Improvement in Cognitive Scores"

Headlines about the 2022 trial often say HBOT "significantly improved" cognition. That's accurate, but the phrase is easy to misread.

Effect size

An effect size of about 0.5 is usually described as moderate. It means the average HBOT participant scored about half a standard deviation better than the average sham participant on the test battery. That's a real, measurable difference on a group level. It is not the same as saying everyone's brain fog lifted.

Group averages versus individuals

Trial results describe averages. Some people in the HBOT group improved a lot, some a little, and some not at all. A study average can't predict how any one person will respond.

Comparison groups improve too

In the Swedish trial, both the HBOT group and the placebo group improved. That pattern is common in conditions where fatigue and symptoms are self-reported. Time, attention, routine and hope all affect how people feel. That is why a sham or placebo group matters so much, and why an uncontrolled registry reporting "improvement" tells us much less than a controlled trial. Cost & Access

Not an FDA-Cleared Indication, and Why That Matters for Cost

Because long COVID is not a cleared indication, Medicare and commercial insurers do not cover HBOT for it. Any clinic offering it is charging self-pay for an investigational use, often for a course of 40 sessions. At the published national range of $150 to $450 per session, that adds up quickly.

If you are searching for HBOT for long COVID in Modesto or anywhere else, here is our honest answer: Generations Wellness does not offer it for this condition. If you are considering it elsewhere, ask the clinic the five questions from our HBOT research overview. Ask which trial their protocol is based on, what pressure and how many sessions they use, and whether a physician will assess you first. Our guide to questions to ask any clinic can help too.

If you want to follow the science, you may also find the same research group's TBI work useful, since several of the long COVID investigators also study brain injury.

Questions

HBOT and Long COVID: Frequently Asked Questions

Does hyperbaric oxygen therapy help long COVID?

One 73-person sham-controlled trial of 40 sessions reported cognitive and symptom improvements; an 80-person trial of 10 sessions found no difference from placebo. It is not a cleared indication, and more trials are needed.

Is HBOT FDA-cleared for long COVID?

No. Hyperbaric chambers are cleared for specific conditions, and long COVID is not one of them. The FDA specifically lists COVID-19 among uses that are not cleared.

How many sessions were used in the trials?

The positive trial used 40 daily sessions at 2.0 ATA. The trial that found no difference used 10 sessions at 2.4 bar.

Is HBOT for long COVID covered by insurance?

No. Investigational uses are not covered by Medicare or commercial plans.

Can I get HBOT for long COVID in Modesto?

Generations Wellness does not offer HBOT for long COVID. Our physician-directed team can discuss the evidence with you and check whether any cleared indication applies.

Are there clinical trials I can join?

Trials have been registered. Search ClinicalTrials.gov for "hyperbaric" and "post-COVID" to see which are currently recruiting; trials deliver treatment under research oversight.
