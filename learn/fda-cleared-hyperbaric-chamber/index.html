What an FDA-Cleared Hyperbaric Chamber Really Means

An FDA-cleared hyperbaric chamber has passed the FDA's 510(k) review, which shows it is substantially equivalent to a chamber already on the market. Clearance covers the device and its labeled uses. It does not mean the FDA has approved hyperbaric oxygen for every condition a clinic advertises.

The Basics

FDA Clearance vs. FDA Approval

Most hyperbaric chambers reach the U.S. market through the FDA's 510(k) premarket notification process. The maker shows the FDA that its chamber is "substantially equivalent" to a device already legally sold. If the FDA agrees, the chamber is cleared.

Clearance is not the same as approval. Approval (premarket approval, or PMA) is a different, more demanding pathway used for higher-risk devices. When you see a chamber described as "FDA-approved," that wording is usually inaccurate.

Just as important, clearance applies to the device and its labeled uses. It does not mean the FDA has endorsed hyperbaric oxygen for every condition a clinic might list. A chamber cleared for recognized medical uses can still be marketed improperly for conditions that are not on that list.

Look It Up

How to Check a Chamber in the FDA 510(k) Database

You can look up clearances yourself in a few minutes:

  1. Open the FDA's free 510(k) Premarket Notification database.
  2. Search by the manufacturer's name, the device name, or the 510(k) number (a "K" followed by six digits) if the clinic gives it to you.
  3. Open the record and read the decision summary or "Indications for Use" statement. This is where the cleared uses are listed.
  4. Compare those uses with what the clinic or seller is claiming.

If a provider cannot tell you the manufacturer or show a clearance, treat that as a warning sign. Soft-shell fabric chambers, for example, are cleared for acute mountain sickness only. See hard-shell vs. soft-shell chambers for why that matters.

Consumer Warning

What the FDA Warns Consumers About HBOT Claims

The FDA's consumer update "Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy: Get the Facts" cautions that some facilities promote HBOT for conditions it has not been cleared or approved to treat. Examples it names include autism, cancer, diabetes, HIV/AIDS, Alzheimer's disease, asthma, Bell's palsy, brain injury, depression, heart disease, migraines, Parkinson's disease, spinal cord injury and stroke.

The agency's concern is twofold. People may delay proven care while trying an unproven option, and HBOT carries real risks, including ear and sinus injury, lung problems, changes in vision, and oxygen toxicity. Fire is also a hazard in an oxygen-rich chamber. Read more on HBOT side effects.

Indications

Uses Recognized by the UHMS

The Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical Society (UHMS) maintains the list of conditions with enough evidence to support HBOT. It includes:

  • Air or gas embolism and decompression sickness
  • Carbon monoxide poisoning
  • Gas gangrene and necrotizing soft tissue infections
  • Crush injury and other acute traumatic reduced blood flow
  • Selected problem wounds, such as certain diabetic foot ulcers
  • Severe anemia when transfusion is not possible
  • Intracranial abscess
  • Chronic bone infection that has not responded to treatment (refractory osteomyelitis)
  • Delayed radiation injury to soft tissue and bone
  • Compromised skin grafts and flaps
  • Acute thermal burns
  • Sudden sensorineural hearing loss
  • Central retinal artery occlusion

Our conditions page explains each one we evaluate. Some of these, such as severe carbon monoxide poisoning or decompression sickness, are emergencies that belong in a hospital.

Watch For

Red Flags in Hyperbaric Marketing

  • Calling a chamber or treatment "FDA-approved"
  • Long lists of conditions not on the UHMS list, especially autism, long COVID, anti-aging or general wellness
  • Promises of specific results or timelines
  • Sessions sold in packages without a physician assessment first
  • Soft-shell chambers described as equal to medical HBOT
  • No clear answer about pressure, oxygen level or who supervises

Questions to Ask a Hyperbaric Clinic

  • Is my condition on the UHMS list of indications?
  • Will a physician assess me before treatment and oversee my care?

Questions

FDA-Cleared Hyperbaric Chambers: Frequently Asked Questions

Are hyperbaric chambers FDA-approved?

Usually not in the strict sense. Most hyperbaric chambers are FDA-cleared through the 510(k) process, which shows they are substantially equivalent to devices already on the market. Approval is a different, more demanding pathway. Clearance covers the device and its labeled uses, not every condition a clinic might advertise, so always check what a chamber is actually cleared for.

How do I find out what a chamber is cleared for?

Search the FDA's free 510(k) Premarket Notification database by manufacturer, device name or 510(k) number. Open the record and read the Indications for Use statement, which lists cleared uses. Then compare it with what the provider claims. A trustworthy clinic will tell you the chamber's manufacturer and help you find this information if you ask.

Is HBOT FDA-cleared for autism or long COVID?

No. The FDA has not cleared hyperbaric chambers for autism, and its consumer update specifically warns about HBOT marketed for autism and many other conditions without clearance. Long COVID is not on the UHMS list of recognized indications either. Research may continue, but those uses are not established, and our practice does not offer HBOT for them.

Are soft-shell chambers FDA-cleared?

Some are, but only for acute mountain sickness. These low-pressure fabric chambers typically reach about 1.3 ATA, usually with room air. They are not cleared for wounds, infections, radiation injury or any of the other recognized medical uses of HBOT. The UHMS does not consider them to deliver hyperbaric oxygen therapy as medically defined.

Does FDA clearance mean HBOT is safe for me?

Not by itself. Clearance means the device met FDA requirements for its labeled uses. Whether HBOT is safe for you depends on your health, such as lung conditions, ear or sinus problems, certain medicines and recent surgery. That is why a physician assessment comes first. Possible side effects include ear pain, sinus pressure, temporary vision changes and, rarely, oxygen toxicity.

What are the FDA-cleared uses of hyperbaric oxygen?

Cleared uses for medical chambers generally follow the UHMS indications. These include decompression sickness, air embolism, carbon monoxide poisoning, certain problem wounds such as diabetic foot ulcers, delayed radiation injury, refractory osteomyelitis, necrotizing infections, compromised grafts and flaps, crush injury, thermal burns, severe anemia, sudden hearing loss and central retinal artery occlusion. Each chamber's exact cleared uses are listed in its 510(k) record.
