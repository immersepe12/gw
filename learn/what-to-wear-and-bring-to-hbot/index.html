What to Wear and Bring to a Hyperbaric Chamber Session (and What to Leave Outside)

Wear 100% cotton, come with clean skin and hair, and leave jewelry, watches and anything electronic with staff. Every rule follows from one fact: a hyperbaric chamber is an oxygen-rich environment, where materials that barely burn in ordinary air can ignite easily. Here is the reason behind each rule.

Fire Safety

Why the Rules Exist: Oxygen and Fire

At Generations Wellness you are treated in a hard-shell monoplace chamber, breathing medical-grade oxygen at the pressure your physician prescribes. Monoplace chambers are pressurized with oxygen, so the space around you is far richer in oxygen than ordinary room air.

Fire needs three things: fuel, heat and oxygen. A chamber supplies plenty of the third, so safety depends on keeping the other two out. That is why the rules focus on:

  • Sparks and heat. Electronics, batteries and anything that can create static or a spark are ignition sources.
  • Fuel. Petroleum-based products, cosmetics, perfumes, hairspray and lotions can burn readily in high oxygen.
  • Fabrics. Cotton is used because it is less prone to static than many synthetic fabrics.

Hyperbaric facilities follow the fire-safety rules in NFPA 99, the Health Care Facilities Code, which has a chapter dedicated to hyperbaric chambers. Chamber fires are very uncommon precisely because these rules are followed every time. The FDA lists fire among the risks of hyperbaric treatment, which is why no exception is made. See our fire-safety procedures and standards.

Clothing

What to Wear in a Hyperbaric Chamber

Wear comfortable, loose, 100% cotton clothing. That is the rule at our center. A few practical tips follow from it:

  • Check the label. Many "cotton" T-shirts and sweatpants are blends with polyester or spandex. If it does not say 100% cotton, ask before you wear it in.
  • Think about every layer. Underwear and socks count too.
  • Leave athletic and fleece fabrics at home. Performance fabrics, fleece and nylon are usually synthetic.
  • Hair accessories. Ask the team about hair ties and clips, and avoid anything metal.

Our team will explain the exact preparation before your first session, including what you can change into if your own clothes do not meet the rule. If you are unsure about any item, call (369) 222-0979 before your session.

Common Questions

Item by Item: Can I Wear or Bring…?

Can I bring my phone into a hyperbaric chamber?

No. Phones, smartwatches, earbuds, tablets and e-readers all contain batteries and circuitry that can spark. They stay outside the chamber with our staff for the whole session.

Can I wear jewelry, a watch or piercings?

No. Jewelry and watches stay outside the chamber with our staff. It is simplest to leave valuables at home on session days. If you have a piercing that is hard to remove, mention it at your assessment.

Can I wear makeup, lotion, deodorant or perfume?

Plan to arrive with clean skin and hair. No petroleum-based products, cosmetics, perfume, hairspray or lotion inside the chamber. That includes things people often forget, such as lip balm, hair gel and scented body sprays. If you are unsure whether a specific product such as a deodorant is acceptable, ask the team rather than guessing.

Can I wear glasses or contact lenses?

Ask the team at your assessment. Policies on eyewear vary between centers, and the answer can depend on the frame material and lens type. If you rely on glasses to see, tell us so we can plan around it.

Can I wear hearing aids?

Hearing aids are electronic devices with batteries, so plan for them to stay outside the chamber. If you depend on them to communicate, tell us beforehand so the team can make sure you can hear them during the session.

Can I keep my dentures in?

Ask the team. Mention dentures, partials and any dental work in progress at your assessment, since pressure changes can occasionally affect teeth with loose fillings.

What about lighters, vapes, matches and hand warmers?

Never. These are direct ignition or heat sources and must not go anywhere near the chamber. Leave them at home or in your car.

Can I keep my CGM, insulin pump or pacemaker?

It depends on the device. Most implanted devices withstand chamber pressure, but published guidance recommends confirming with the manufacturer. Wearable devices such as continuous glucose monitors and insulin pumps also contain electronics, so they need to be discussed before your first session. Bring your device card or model information to your assessment, and never remove an insulin pump without a plan from your diabetes care team. See pacemakers and implanted devices and tell us about your device in advance.

Can I read or watch TV during the session?

Paper and electronics are both potential fire concerns, and what is allowed varies by center. Ask the team what you can use to pass the time. Many people simply rest or nap.

Can I bring water or a snack into the chamber?

Plan to eat and drink before and after your session rather than inside. Ask the team if you have a medical reason to need something during a session.

In Your Bag

What to Bring to Your Appointment

These stay with you or with staff outside the chamber, but they make your visit smoother:

  • Photo ID, especially for your first visit.
  • A current medication list, including past chemotherapy and anything new.
  • Records from your other doctors, if you have them, especially if a physician suggested HBOT.
  • Device information for any implanted or wearable medical device.
  • Your glucose meter and a snack if you have diabetes.
  • A 100% cotton layer in case the room feels cool.
  • Your questions. Write them down so you do not forget.

For how the rest of the day goes, read what happens at your first session.

Before You Come

Eating, Coffee, Medications and Smoking Before a Session

  • Eat normally. Have your usual meal before a session unless your physician tells you otherwise. A long session on an empty stomach is uncomfortable, and for people with diabetes it matters more.
  • Coffee. Ask your physician whether your usual coffee or tea is fine for you on session days.
  • Medications. Keep taking your regular medications unless your physician says otherwise. Tell the team about any new prescription before your next session, because a few medicines, such as disulfiram and certain chemotherapy drugs, matter under pressure.
  • Smoking and vaping. Never bring lighters or vapes near the chamber. If you smoke, talk to your physician about it during your course.
  • Feeling unwell. If you have a cold, fever or congestion, call before you come. See clearing your ears and why congestion matters.

Diabetes

If You Have Diabetes

Many people who receive HBOT have diabetes, often because of diabetic foot ulcer care. Low blood sugar during treatment is a recognized consideration, and published guidance describes managing it with glucose monitoring. Read more about hypoglycemia and other side effects during sessions.

Before your first session, the team will explain your plan for treatment days. In the meantime: tell the physician exactly how your diabetes is managed, bring your meter and a snack, do not skip meals unless told to, and speak up right away if you feel shaky, sweaty or confused during a session.

Your Session-Day Checklist

  • 100% cotton clothing, including underwear and socks.
  • Clean skin and hair: no lotion, makeup, perfume, hairspray or petroleum-based products.
  • Jewelry and watches left at home or with staff.
  • Phone, earbuds and other electronics handed to staff.
  • No lighters, vapes or hand warmers.
  • Glasses, contacts, hearing aids, dentures: checked with the team.
  • Medical devices discussed before your first session.
  • A normal meal beforehand, unless told otherwise.
  • Regular medications taken, and any new ones reported.
  • Glucose meter and snack if you have diabetes.
  • A call ahead if you have a cold, fever or congestion.
  • Ear-clearing plan: swallow early and often, and speak up about pain.

Important

A physician assessment is required before beginning HBOT at Generations Wellness. HBOT complements, and never replaces, the treatment prescribed by your doctors.

Questions

What to Wear and Bring: Frequently Asked Questions

More short answers are in our HBOT FAQ.

What should I wear in a hyperbaric chamber?

Comfortable, 100% cotton clothing with no synthetic blends, and nothing on your skin or hair. Jewelry, watches and electronics stay outside with staff.

Why can't you wear lotion or deodorant?

Petroleum-based products and many cosmetics can act as fuel in an oxygen-rich chamber. Ask the team before using any specific product on a session day.

Can you bring your phone into a hyperbaric chamber?

No. Phones and all other electronics can spark, so they stay outside the chamber with our staff.

Can I watch TV or read?

It depends on the center's fire-safety policy, so ask the team what is available. Many people rest or nap.

Can I eat or drink coffee before HBOT?

Eat your normal meal unless your physician advises otherwise. Ask your physician whether your usual coffee is fine for you on session days.

Can I keep my CGM or insulin pump on?

It depends on the device and its manufacturer's guidance. Tell us in advance and bring the model information so it can be checked before your first session.
