Comparing Options

Private vs. School Speech Therapy: Which Fits Your Child?

School speech therapy through an IEP is free for children who qualify and focuses on needs that affect learning at school. Private speech therapy is paid for by families or their health plans and can focus on any communication goal. Many families use both, and for children under 3, California Early Start comes first.

Birth to 3

Under 3: California Early Start

For babies and toddlers, schools aren't usually involved yet. California Early Start is the state's early intervention program for children from birth to 36 months with, or at risk for, developmental delays. It runs through regional centers; in Stanislaus County, that is Valley Mountain Regional Center.

  • Anyone can make a referral, including parents. You don't need a doctor's order.
  • Federal early intervention rules call for an evaluation and, if your child is eligible, an Individualized Family Service Plan (IFSP) within 45 days of referral.
  • Services often happen in natural settings, like your home, and focus on coaching the family.

As your child nears age 3, the Early Start team helps plan the transition to your school district.

Age 3 and Up

School Speech Therapy and IEP Speech Services

Under the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), public school districts must find, evaluate and serve children with disabilities from age 3, including private-school and homeschooled children in the district. Speech or language impairment is one of the eligibility categories.

How to request a school assessment

Write to your school or district special education office asking for a speech and language assessment, and keep a copy. In California, the district generally must give you an assessment plan within 15 days, and then complete the assessment and hold an IEP meeting within 60 days of receiving your signed consent, not counting certain school breaks. Ask your district about current timelines, since rules can change.

What school services focus on

To qualify, a child's communication difficulty must affect their education, which can include academics, social participation and classroom communication. Services are written into an IEP with goals, minutes and setting. Sessions are often in small groups, sometimes in the classroom, during the school day.

Strengths and limits

School services are free, happen where your child already is, and connect directly with teachers. On the other hand, a child with a mild speech difference that doesn't affect school performance may not qualify, group sizes and minutes vary, and goals focus on school needs.

Outside School

Private Speech Therapy

Private speech therapy happens at a clinic like ours, with a licensed speech-language pathologist. It is not tied to school eligibility rules, so it can address any communication goal your family and the SLP agree on.

  • Often one-on-one, with sessions planned around your child's specific goals.
  • Parents are usually in the room or nearby and coached on home practice.
  • Can start without school eligibility, for example during a wait for school or Regional Center services, or for a child who didn't qualify.
  • Has a cost. Prices vary widely by region, session length and whether a health plan contributes. Some health plans cover speech therapy when it's medically necessary; check your plan's terms.

Questions to ask any private provider

  • Is the therapist a licensed speech-language pathologist in California?
  • How will goals be set, and how will progress be measured and shared with me?
  • How will I be involved, and what will we practice at home?
  • Will you coordinate with my child's school team, pediatrician or regional center if I give permission?
  • What are the costs for evaluation and ongoing sessions, and are there other charges?

At our practice, call us to ask about current pricing and payment options; we review costs with you before treatment. See self-pay pricing for more.

Combining Care

Using School and Private Speech Therapy Together

Many families do both. It can work well when everyone is pulling in the same direction:

  • Share reports between your school team and private SLP, with your written permission.
  • Ask both therapists to align goals so your child practices the same skills in different places.
  • Bring your private SLP's report to IEP meetings if it adds useful information. The school must consider outside evaluations you share, though it doesn't have to adopt their recommendations.

If you disagree with a school evaluation, California families have the right to ask for an independent educational evaluation and to use dispute resolution options. Your district's special education office and the California Department of Education can explain these procedural safeguards.

Private therapy does not replace your child's rights at school. Even if you pay for private therapy, you can still ask your district for an assessment and services.

Families from Modesto, Ceres, Turlock and across Stanislaus County can book an in-person speech evaluation at our downtown practice. Call (369) 222-0979 or (209) 809-0022, or read about our pediatric speech therapy.

Important

Whichever route you choose, an assessment by a licensed clinician comes before a treatment plan. Program rules and timelines described here reflect our understanding as of 2026 and can change; confirm details with your regional center or school district.

Questions

School and Private Speech Therapy: Frequently Asked Questions

Is school speech therapy enough?

For many children, yes. Others benefit from extra private sessions, especially if their goals reach beyond school or progress is slow. Your SLP can help you decide. School services are free, happen where your child already is and connect with teachers. Private therapy is often one-on-one and can address any goal your family and the SLP agree on.

How much does speech therapy cost for a child?

Early Start and school IEP services are free for eligible children. Private costs vary by provider and health plan; call us for our current pricing. For children under 3, anyone can make an Early Start referral, including parents, and you don't need a doctor's order. In Stanislaus County, Early Start runs through Valley Mountain Regional Center.

My child didn't qualify at school. What now?

Ask the district to explain the decision in writing. A private evaluation can give a second view, and private therapy can address needs that don't meet school criteria. A child with a mild speech difference that doesn't affect school performance may not qualify for school services. Private therapy can start without school eligibility.

Can my child get speech therapy at school and privately?

Yes. Many families do both, and sharing reports between therapists helps keep goals aligned. Private sessions can add one-on-one time and home practice coaching for parents. School sessions are often in small groups during the school day. Your SLP can help plan how the two fit together for your child.

Do public schools provide speech therapy?

Yes, if your child qualifies. Under the federal special education law (IDEA), public schools evaluate children ages 3 to 21 at no cost and provide speech therapy when a speech or language problem affects learning. Services are written into an IEP. In California, children under 3 can be evaluated through Early Start. Private therapy is an option if your child does not qualify or needs more support.
