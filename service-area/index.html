Service Area

Serving Stanislaus County and the Central Valley

One Center, a Wide Region

Why the Drive Matters for HBOT

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is rarely a one-time visit. For the conditions HBOT is cleared for, a physician commonly recommends a course of 20 to 40 sessions, usually one a day, five days a week. Each session runs about 90 to 120 minutes including the time it takes to pressurize and depressurize the chamber. Add your drive, and the trip becomes part of your daily routine for several weeks.

That is why we think the distance deserves an honest look before you start. Our center is in downtown Modesto, which puts it close to the middle of Stanislaus County and within reach of San Joaquin and Merced county towns along CA-99. For some patients the drive is a short hop. For others it takes real planning. This page helps you work out which describes you, and our team is happy to talk it through when you call.

Modesto and its neighbors

If you live in Modesto, Ceres or Salida, the drive downtown is usually short, and a daily course tends to fit around work and family without much trouble. Ceres sits just south of Modesto along CA-99, and Salida sits just to the north.

Towns along CA-99 to the north

Ripon lies on CA-99 between Manteca and Modesto, so Ripon patients can use the Manteca guide as a starting point and expect a somewhat shorter drive. Tracy is farther west, reached by way of I-205 and CA-120 before joining CA-99, so plan on a longer trip than from Manteca.

Towns to the east and west

Riverbank and Oakdale are east of Modesto, reached by local roads and CA-108 rather than CA-99. Patterson is to the southwest, on the other side of the county near I-5. For any of these towns, check a map app at the time of day you would travel, since conditions change through the day.

Planning a Course

Is a 20 to 40 Session Course Realistic From Your City?

A useful rule of thumb is to add your round-trip drive to about two hours for the session itself and check-in. Then ask whether you can repeat that most weekdays for four to eight weeks. Here is how that tends to play out:

  • Under about 25 minutes each way (Modesto, Ceres, Salida, Turlock, Manteca, Ripon). A daily course is usually routine. Many people keep the same appointment time each day.
  • About 25 to 45 minutes each way (Stockton, Merced and similar distances). A daily course is workable with a plan. A consistent slot outside commute peaks, a family driver rotation, or pairing sessions with errands in Modesto can help.
  • Longer than that. Talk to us before you commit. We can discuss scheduling, and we will tell you honestly if a program closer to home may suit you better. Our guide to hospital-based and independent HBOT centers explains the difference.

To see what the time in the chamber actually involves, read how long a session takes and what happens during it.

Directions

Getting Here by Corridor

From CA-99 (Turlock, Ceres, Salida, Ripon, Manteca, Stockton, Merced)

Most of our patients arrive on CA-99. Follow it into Modesto, take the downtown exits, and head to H Street. We are at 1801 H Street, Suite C-1. Your map app will pick the best exit for current traffic.

From I-5, I-205 and CA-120 (Tracy, Lathrop, Patterson)

From Tracy and the west side of San Joaquin County, most routes use I-205 and CA-120 to reach CA-99 at Manteca, then continue south. From Patterson, you will cross the county toward Modesto on local highways.

From the east (Riverbank, Oakdale)

From Riverbank and Oakdale, local roads and CA-108 bring you west into Modesto without using CA-99.

Parking and arrival

Street parking and nearby lot parking are available. Sessions are by appointment, so please call ahead to schedule. If you use a wheelchair or walker, or need help getting in and out of the car, let us know when you book so we can plan for your arrival. How We Work With Your Local Doctors

You don't need to change doctors to receive HBOT with us. Care at Generations Wellness is directed by Dr. Rajwinder Singh Bahia, MD, and every course begins with a physician assessment. With your permission, we share that assessment and your progress with the doctors who already look after you, whether they are in Modesto, Turlock, Stockton, Merced or elsewhere. That way HBOT fits alongside the wound care, cancer care or specialist care you already receive. Physicians can read how we work with referring physicians.

  • Hard-shell monoplace chambers. You are treated alone in a single-person chamber, breathing medical-grade oxygen at a physician-prescribed pressure.
  • A physician assessment first. Nobody starts a course until a physician has reviewed their history and whether HBOT is appropriate.
  • Clear self-pay pricing. We currently offer HBOT on a self-pay basis and review pricing with you at your consultation. See what HBOT costs.

New to hyperbaric medicine? Start with what HBOT is and how it works at our center.

Important

A physician assessment is required before beginning HBOT at Generations Wellness. HBOT complements, and never replaces, the treatment prescribed by your doctors. We are an outpatient center and do not provide emergency treatment; in an emergency, call 911.

Questions

Service Area: Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a hyperbaric chamber in Modesto?

Yes. Generations Wellness provides HBOT in hard-shell monoplace chambers at 1801 H Street, Suite C-1, in downtown Modesto. Every course is directed by a physician and starts with a physician assessment.

How far is Generations Wellness from Turlock, Manteca, Stockton or Merced?

Typical off-peak drives are about 19 minutes from Turlock, 21 from Manteca, 36 from Stockton and 40 from Merced, mostly via CA-99. Times vary with traffic.

Do you have locations in other cities?

No. We have one center, in downtown Modesto. With your permission, we keep your doctors in your own city informed about your care.

Do I need a referral to book from outside Modesto?

You can call us yourself, or your doctor can contact us. Either way, a physician assessment comes before any course, wherever you live.

Can I get HBOT for an emergency here?

No. We are an outpatient center that sees patients by appointment. For emergencies such as carbon monoxide poisoning or a diving injury, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.
