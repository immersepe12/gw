Neck Pain

Neck Pain Physical Therapy

Physical therapy is a common, guideline-supported treatment for neck pain, including stiffness from desk and phone use and whiplash after a car accident. A licensed physical therapist combines exercise, hands-on care and practical habit changes to help you turn your head, work and sleep with less pain.

Safety First

Neck Pain Warning Signs

Call 911 or go to an emergency room for neck pain with:

  • A serious crash, fall or blow to the head, especially with numbness, tingling or weakness
  • Fever with a stiff neck and headache, or sensitivity to light
  • Sudden severe headache, dizziness, double vision, trouble speaking or swallowing, or weakness on one side
  • Trouble walking, clumsy hands or loss of bladder or bowel control

See a doctor before PT if pain spreads down an arm with numbness or weakness, or if you have unexplained weight loss or a history of cancer. Our physician can evaluate you first if needed.

Everyday Neck Pain

Tech Neck: Neck Pain From Phones and Desks

Most neck pain is "mechanical": it comes from the muscles, joints and discs of the neck, and it changes with position and movement. Long hours looking down at a phone, laptop or paperwork are common triggers, which is why people call it "tech neck." Driving long distances, overhead work and poor sleep can add to it.

There is no single "perfect" posture. The bigger problem is staying in any one position too long. Helpful changes include:

  • Raising your screen so the top is about at eye level, and holding your phone higher
  • Keeping the keyboard close so your elbows rest near your sides
  • Setting a reminder to change position or stand every 30 to 45 minutes
  • Using a pillow that keeps your neck roughly level with your body when you sleep

These changes help, but exercise is usually the bigger part of care. Strengthening the deep neck and upper-back muscles improves how long you can hold positions comfortably.

After a Car Accident

Physical Therapy for Whiplash

Whiplash is a neck injury caused by a quick back-and-forth movement of the head, most often in a rear-end car crash. Symptoms can include neck pain and stiffness, headaches starting at the base of the skull, shoulder pain, tiredness and sometimes dizziness. They may not show up until a day or two after the accident.

Get checked by a physician first after any crash. Once serious injury has been ruled out, guidelines generally favor staying active and starting gentle movement early over wearing a soft collar for long periods. Physical therapy for whiplash usually includes:

  • Reassurance and education about what is normal while healing
  • Gentle range-of-motion exercises, progressing to strengthening
  • Hands-on techniques when they help you move more comfortably
  • Gradual return to driving, work and exercise

Many people improve within weeks to a few months, though some have symptoms that last longer. Stress and worry after a crash can affect pain, and that's normal to talk about; our practice also offers counseling as part of a shared care plan. For other accident-related injuries, see our sports, work and auto injury guide.

Your Plan

What Neck Pain Physical Therapy Involves

The evaluation

Your therapist asks when the pain started, what triggers it, and whether you get headaches, arm symptoms or dizziness. They check neck and upper-back motion, strength, nerve function in your arms, and how your shoulders move. Neck and shoulder problems often overlap; see shoulder pain physical therapy.

Treatment

  • Exercise. Deep neck flexor training, shoulder blade strengthening and mobility work. Clinical practice guidelines support exercise for most types of neck pain.
  • Manual therapy. Joint mobilization and soft-tissue techniques for the neck and upper back, often combined with exercise.
  • Work and home changes. Practical setup changes for your desk, car and bed.
  • Headache strategies. If your headaches start in the neck, treatment may target those muscles and joints. For migraines and other headache types, see our migraine and headache guide.

What you can do now

Keep moving your neck gently within comfort, use heat if it relaxes tight muscles, and break up long sitting or screen time. Avoid cracking or forcing your neck. Ask a clinician before starting new pain medicines if you have health conditions or take other medicines.

Questions

Neck Pain Physical Therapy: Frequently Asked Questions

Is physical therapy good for neck pain?

For most mechanical neck pain, yes. Guidelines support exercise, often combined with manual therapy, as a core treatment. Exercise may include deep neck flexor training, shoulder blade strengthening and mobility work. Manual therapy includes joint mobilization and soft-tissue techniques for the neck and upper back. Your therapist assesses you before building a plan.

How long does whiplash take to heal?

Many people improve within weeks to a few months, but recovery varies and some have longer-lasting symptoms. Staying active usually helps. Keep moving your neck gently within comfort, use heat if it relaxes tight muscles, and break up long sitting or screen time. Avoid cracking or forcing your neck.

Can bad posture cause neck pain?

Staying in one position for a long time, especially looking down, can contribute. Moving often and building strength usually matter more than holding a perfect posture. Try raising your screen so the top is about at eye level, and set a reminder to change position or stand every 30 to 45 minutes.

Should I wear a neck brace after whiplash?

Only if a physician tells you to. For typical whiplash without serious injury, early gentle movement is generally favored over long-term collar use. Get checked by a physician first after any crash. Whiplash PT usually includes gentle range-of-motion exercises, then strengthening and a gradual return to driving, work and exercise.

Can physical therapy help a pinched nerve in the neck?

Often, yes. Arm pain from an irritated nerve root (cervical radiculopathy) frequently improves over weeks to months with exercise, nerve mobility work and position changes. Growing weakness or numbness needs a physician's evaluation right away. Your therapist checks nerve function in your arms during the evaluation. A physician in the same practice can evaluate you first if needed.

What is the best sleeping position for neck pain?

Sleeping on your back or side with a pillow that keeps your neck level is usually most comfortable. Stomach sleeping twists the neck and often aggravates pain. Poor sleep can add to neck pain. Your therapist can suggest practical setup changes for your bed, as well as your desk and car.
