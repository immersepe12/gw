Post-Surgical Rehab

Post-Surgical Rehab and Physical Therapy in Modesto

After orthopedic surgery, outpatient physical therapy helps you regain motion, strength and confidence. Our licensed physical therapists follow your surgeon's protocol and precautions, track your progress at each visit, and keep your surgeon informed, so you can get back to walking, working and daily life.

Safety First

Warning Signs After Surgery

Call 911 for chest pain or sudden shortness of breath. Contact your surgeon the same day if you notice:

  • Fever or chills
  • Increasing redness, warmth, swelling or drainage from the incision, or a bad smell
  • A calf that is newly swollen, tender, red or warm
  • Pain that suddenly gets much worse, or a joint that feels like it has slipped out of place

Your physical therapist checks for these at each visit and will contact your surgeon or send you for care if something isn't right.

The Basics

How Outpatient Rehab After Surgery Works

Rehab often starts in the hospital, sometimes the same day as surgery. Many people then continue at home for a short time and move on to outpatient physical therapy once they can travel to a clinic. Your surgeon decides when that happens.

Your surgeon's protocol comes first

Most surgeons give a written protocol: how much weight you can put on the leg, which movements to avoid, and when to add new exercises. Your physical therapist follows it and checks with your surgeon's office if anything is unclear. Bring the protocol and your discharge papers to your first visit.

What outpatient rehab focuses on

  • Range of motion. Getting the joint to bend and straighten as far as the surgery allows.
  • Strength. Rebuilding muscles that weaken quickly after surgery and time off your feet.
  • Walking and balance. Moving from walker to cane to walking without help, when safe.
  • Swelling and pain management. Positioning, icing schedules and activity pacing.
  • Daily tasks. Stairs, getting into a car, getting off the floor, returning to work or sport.

A note on California's direct access rule: if you arrive without a referral, a physical therapist can treat you for up to 45 days or 12 visits before a physician signs the plan. After surgery, most patients already have a referral or prescription from their surgeon. Read more about physical therapy without a referral.

Total Knee Replacement

Physical Therapy After Knee Replacement

Knee replacement rehab is hard work, and it matters. The early weeks focus on getting the knee straight and bending it far enough for daily life. Many people need roughly 90 degrees of bend for stairs and around 105 to 110 degrees to get up easily from a chair, though needs vary.

  • Early phase: walking with a walker or cane, swelling control, heel slides and quad sets, and fully straightening the knee.
  • Middle phase: more bend, step-ups, balance work, and longer walks.
  • Later phase: strength for stairs, kneeling where your surgeon allows, and getting back to hobbies such as gardening, golf or travel.

The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons notes that most people resume ordinary daily activities within about three to six weeks after knee replacement, while full recovery and strength gains continue for months. Everyone heals at a different pace, so no fixed timeline can be promised. Stiffness is common; keep up with home exercises between visits, because missed work in the early weeks is hard to make up later.

Total Hip Replacement

Physical Therapy After Hip Replacement

Recovery after hip replacement is often quicker than after knee replacement, but the hip still needs focused rehab. Depending on the surgical approach, your surgeon may give "hip precautions," such as limits on bending past 90 degrees, crossing your legs or turning your foot inward. Your therapist teaches you how to dress, sit, use the toilet and get in and out of a car within those limits.

Rehab builds hip and core strength, improves walking pattern, and works on balance to lower fall risk. As precautions are lifted, you progress to stairs, longer walks and your usual activities.

Sports Surgery

ACL Reconstruction Rehab

ACL rehab is long. Return to cutting, jumping and pivoting sports commonly takes nine months or more, and many surgeons and research groups advise not returning before that point. Rehab moves through phases:

  1. Getting the knee fully straight, controlling swelling and waking up the quadriceps
  2. Normal walking, then progressive strength and balance work
  3. Running, jumping and landing drills once strength and control allow
  4. Sport-specific training and return-to-sport testing, decided with your surgeon

Strength and hop tests help guide the decision, not the calendar alone. For non-surgical sports injuries, see our sports injury physical therapy guide.

Other Procedures

Rehab After Shoulder, Spine and Other Surgery

  • Rotator cuff repair and shoulder surgery. Early protection in a sling, then gradual motion and strengthening over months. See shoulder pain physical therapy.
  • Spine surgery. Walking, gentle core work and safe movement habits, within your surgeon's limits.
  • Foot and ankle surgery. Restoring motion, balance and walking once weight-bearing is allowed.
  • Hand and wrist surgery. Often handled by occupational therapy; see hand therapy.

Before and After

How to Prepare for Rehab

  • Consider "prehab." If your surgery is planned, ask your surgeon whether a few weeks of exercise beforehand makes sense. Stronger muscles going in may help you coming out.
  • Set up your home. Clear walkways, remove loose rugs, add night lights, and keep everyday items at waist height.
  • Arrange rides. You may not be allowed to drive for a while after surgery.
  • Plan your pain medicine timing. Your surgeon may suggest taking prescribed pain medicine before therapy. Follow their instructions exactly.
  • Bring the paperwork. Protocol, precautions, operative note if you have it, and a medicine list.

Getting the most from rehab

  • Do the home program every day. Clinic visits guide your recovery, but most of the gains come from the exercises you do at home.
  • Sleep and eat well. Healing tissue needs rest and enough protein. If you have diabetes, keeping blood sugar steady supports wound healing.
  • Speak up. Tell your therapist about new pain, poor sleep, low mood or worries about falling. Each one can change the plan.
  • Expect ups and downs. Good weeks and stiff weeks are both normal. Look at progress over several weeks, not day to day.

Questions

Post-Surgical Rehab: Frequently Asked Questions

How long is physical therapy after knee replacement?

Formal outpatient PT often lasts several weeks to a few months, and strength keeps improving for longer. Your surgeon and therapist set the plan based on your progress.

When can I start outpatient PT after surgery?

Your surgeon decides. Many people begin outpatient therapy within the first couple of weeks after joint replacement, once they can travel safely.

Is it normal for rehab to hurt?

Some discomfort is expected, especially when working on bend and stiffness. Sudden, severe or worsening pain is not; tell your therapist and surgeon.

When can I return to sports after ACL surgery?

Commonly not before about nine months, and only after passing strength and movement tests and getting your surgeon's clearance.

Do I need a referral for rehab after surgery?

California law lets you start PT without one, within limits, but after surgery your surgeon's protocol and orders guide care, so bring them to your first visit.
