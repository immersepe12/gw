The Basics

What Outpatient Physical Therapy Is

Physical therapy (PT) helps you move better, hurt less and get back to the things you need and want to do. A licensed physical therapist looks at how your body moves, finds what is limiting you, and teaches you exercises and strategies to improve strength, flexibility, balance and control. The American Physical Therapy Association describes physical therapists as movement experts who treat people of all ages through hands-on care, exercise and patient education.

"Outpatient" means you come in for a visit, work with your therapist, and go home the same day. At Generations Wellness Medical Practice, every visit is outpatient and by appointment. We do not provide inpatient or in-home care.

What a typical visit includes

  • Therapeutic exercise. Exercises chosen for your problem, progressed as you get stronger.
  • Hands-on techniques. Your therapist may use manual therapy to help stiff joints and tight tissue move more easily, when that fits your plan.
  • Movement retraining. Practice with the real tasks that hurt, like lifting, climbing stairs, reaching overhead or getting up from a chair.
  • Education. What is going on, what helps, what to avoid for now, and how to manage flare-ups.
  • A home program. Most progress happens between visits, so you leave with exercises you can do at home.

PT is active care. You do the work, and your therapist guides it. That is a big reason it can help people stay independent and avoid relying only on medication for pain.

Direct Access

Do You Need a Referral for Physical Therapy in California?

Usually not to get started. Under California law, as of 2026 (Business and Professions Code section 2620.1), you can see a licensed physical therapist directly, without a doctor's referral. This is called direct access.

There is a limit. A physical therapist can treat you without a referral for up to 45 calendar days or 12 visits, whichever comes first. To keep going after that, a physician or surgeon (or a podiatrist, within their scope) must sign and date your plan of care after examining you. Your therapist must also refer you to a physician if your symptoms point to something outside physical therapy.

Because Generations Wellness is a physician-led practice, that sign-off step can happen within the same practice that already knows your history. It is one less handoff for you to manage.

Two cautions: rules can change, so check the current law if timing matters to you, and some health plans set their own referral rules no matter what state law allows. For the full explanation, read physical therapy without a referral in California.

What to Expect

Your First Physical Therapy Visit

Your first visit is an evaluation. It is mostly talking and testing, with some treatment if there is time. Plan for it to take longer than later visits.

  1. Your story. When the problem started, what makes it better or worse, past injuries, surgeries, medicines and what you want to get back to doing.
  2. Screening. Questions and checks to make sure PT is safe and appropriate, and that nothing needs a doctor's attention first.
  3. Movement testing. Range of motion, strength, balance, walking and the specific movements that bother you.
  4. Findings in plain words. What your therapist thinks is going on and why.
  5. A plan. Goals you agree on, how often you may come in, and your first home exercises.

Wear clothes you can move in. If your knee hurts, shorts help; for a shoulder, a loose top or tank. Bring a list of your medicines, any imaging reports or surgical notes, and your surgeon's protocol if you had an operation. Our new patient guide lists what to bring.

How many visits will I need?

It depends on the problem, how long you've had it, your goals and how consistently you do your home program. Some people need a handful of visits to learn a self-management plan. Recovery after a joint replacement usually takes longer. Your therapist reviews progress regularly and adjusts the plan; nobody can promise a fixed timeline.

  • A physician in the same practice. Care is directed by Dr. Rajwinder Singh Bahia, MD (internal medicine), which simplifies the plan-of-care sign-off that California requires after 45 days or 12 visits.

This matters most when pain has more than one cause. Long-lasting back pain, for example, often improves best with a mix of exercise, education, sleep, stress support and careful medication choices. Having those pieces in one practice makes the plan easier to follow.

Safety First

When to See a Doctor Before Physical Therapy

Most aches and strains are safe to bring straight to a physical therapist. Some symptoms are not. Call 911 or go to an emergency room if you have:

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin or inner thighs
  • Sudden weakness in an arm or leg, trouble speaking or a drooping face
  • Back or neck pain with fever, or after a serious fall or crash
  • Chest pain or trouble breathing
  • A joint that looks deformed, or a calf that is suddenly swollen, warm and painful

See a doctor soon, before starting PT, if you have pain that wakes you every night, unexplained weight loss, a history of cancer with new pain, or symptoms that keep getting worse. Our physician can evaluate you first if needed.

Questions

Physical Therapy in Modesto: Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a doctor's referral for physical therapy in Modesto?

Usually not to start. Under California law, as of 2026, a physical therapist can treat you without a referral for up to 45 calendar days or 12 visits; after that a physician must sign your plan of care. Your health plan may have its own rules.

How long is a physical therapy session?

The first visit is an evaluation and usually takes longer than follow-up visits. We'll tell you how long to plan for when you book.

What should I wear to physical therapy?

Comfortable clothes you can move in, and supportive shoes. Choose clothing that lets your therapist see and reach the area being treated, such as shorts for a knee.

Does physical therapy hurt?

Some exercises can feel uncomfortable or leave you mildly sore, much like starting any new activity. Sharp or worsening pain is not the goal; tell your therapist so the plan can be adjusted.
