Ankle Sprain

Ankle Sprain Physical Therapy in Modesto

Ankle sprains are among the most common injuries, and many people never fully rehab them. Physical therapy helps a sprained ankle heal well by restoring motion, strength and balance, which lowers the chance of spraining it again. A licensed physical therapist guides you from the first sore days back to walking, work and sport.

Safety First

Sprain vs Fracture: When You Need an X-Ray

A sprain is a stretch or tear of the ligaments that hold the ankle bones together. Most happen when the foot rolls inward. A fracture is a break in the bone. They can feel similar, so doctors often use the Ottawa ankle rules to decide who needs an X-ray. An X-ray is usually recommended if you have pain around the ankle bones and either:

  • Tenderness along the back edge or tip of either ankle bone (the bumps on the inside and outside of the ankle)
  • Tenderness at the base of the fifth metatarsal (the outer edge of the midfoot) or the navicular bone (inner midfoot)
  • You could not take four steps right after the injury and still cannot in the clinic

These rules are a tool for clinicians, not a home test. Get same-day medical care or go to urgent care if the ankle looks deformed, you cannot put weight on it, your foot is numb, cold or pale, or there is an open wound. Our physician can evaluate you before therapy if needed.

Recovery

How Long Does a Sprained Ankle Take to Heal?

Sprains are graded by how much the ligament is damaged:

  • Grade 1 (mild). Stretched ligament, mild swelling. Many people walk comfortably within a week or two.
  • Grade 2 (moderate). Partial tear, more swelling and bruising. Recovery often takes several weeks.
  • Grade 3 (severe). Full tear, the ankle may feel unstable. Recovery can take a few months.

These are rough ranges, not promises. Ligaments keep remodeling for months after the pain fades, which is one reason the ankle can stay weak and wobbly if you skip rehab. Current guidance favors early, protected movement over long rest. A short period of a brace or support can help in the first weeks.

Your Plan

What Ankle Sprain Physical Therapy Involves

Early phase

In the first days, the focus is on easing swelling and protecting the ankle while keeping it moving. That often means elevation, compression, gentle ankle circles and walking as comfort allows, with a brace or crutches if needed.

Strength and motion

As pain settles, your therapist adds exercises to restore ankle bend and strength: heel raises, resistance band work in all directions, and calf stretching. Hands-on joint techniques may help stiff ankles move more freely.

Balance training

A sprain damages small nerve sensors in the ligaments that tell your brain where your foot is. Balance training retrains that sense. Research supports exercise and balance work after an ankle sprain to lower the risk of re-injury. Typical progressions include:

  • Standing on one leg, first with eyes open, then closed
  • Balancing on a pillow or foam pad
  • Reaching with the other foot in different directions while balancing
  • Hopping, cutting and landing drills for athletes

Return to activity

Before you return to sport or demanding work, your therapist tests hopping, balance and side-to-side movement. Bracing or taping during sport for several months is often suggested after a sprain. For other athletic injuries, see our sports injury physical therapy page.

Special Cases

High Ankle Sprains and Recurrent Sprains

High ankle sprain

A high ankle sprain injures the ligaments that join the two lower leg bones just above the ankle. It often happens when the foot twists outward, as in football or soccer. Pain sits above the ankle and gets worse when you turn your foot outward or push off. High ankle sprains usually take longer to recover than common sprains, and some need a physician or surgeon to check stability.

Recurrent sprains and chronic ankle instability

Many people who sprain an ankle go on to sprain it again, and some develop chronic ankle instability: a feeling that the ankle gives way on uneven ground. Strength and balance training help many people. If the ankle keeps giving way despite good rehab, a physician or orthopedic surgeon can discuss other options. Older adults with ankle weakness and falls may also benefit from our balance and fall prevention program.

Questions

Ankle Sprain Physical Therapy: Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a sprained ankle take to heal?

It depends on the grade. Mild sprains often feel much better within one to two weeks, moderate sprains can take several weeks, and severe sprains may take a few months. Ligaments keep healing after pain fades, so finishing your strength and balance exercises matters. Timelines vary, and your therapist tracks progress with you.

How do I know if my ankle is broken or sprained?

You often can't tell on your own. Clinicians use the Ottawa ankle rules, which check for tenderness at specific spots on the ankle and foot bones and whether you can take four steps. If you cannot bear weight, the ankle looks deformed, or bone points are very tender, get seen the same day for an X-ray.

Do I need physical therapy for a sprained ankle?

Not everyone does, but many people benefit, especially after moderate or severe sprains, repeat sprains, or if you play sports. Rehab restores strength and balance, and research shows exercise-based care lowers the risk of spraining it again. Even a few visits can give you a home program to finish recovery.

What is a high ankle sprain?

A high ankle sprain injures the ligaments that connect the two lower leg bones just above the ankle joint. It usually happens when the foot twists outward. Pain sits higher than a typical sprain and recovery often takes longer. A clinician should check stability, since some high ankle sprains need a surgeon's opinion.

Why do I keep spraining the same ankle?

After a sprain, ligaments may stay stretched and the ankle's balance sense is weaker, so it can roll more easily. Without rehab, the cycle can repeat. Targeted balance and strength training helps many people. If your ankle keeps giving way despite good rehab, a physician or orthopedic surgeon can review other options with you.
