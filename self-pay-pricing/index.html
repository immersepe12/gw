Cost & Payment

How Self-Pay Pricing Works

If you pay for care yourself, federal law gives you the right to a written Good Faith Estimate before your visit. Here is how estimates work, how HSA and FSA funds are generally used, what a superbill is, and what affects the cost of each service at our Modesto practice.

Generations Wellness Medical Practice · Medical director Dr. Rajwinder Singh Bahia, MD · Updated October 24, 2026

Your Rights

Your Right to a Good Faith Estimate

Under the federal No Surprises Act, which took effect in 2022, health care providers must give patients who are uninsured, or who choose not to use insurance, a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges. You don't have to ask in a special way, and the estimate is free.

When you get it

  • If you schedule care at least 3 business days ahead, you should get the estimate within 1 business day of scheduling.
  • If you schedule at least 10 business days ahead, you should get it within 3 business days.
  • If you simply ask about costs, you should get it within 3 business days of asking.

What it includes

The estimate is written and itemized. It lists the items and services reasonably expected for your care, with expected charges. For a course of care, like a series of therapy visits, an estimate can cover expected visits for up to 12 months.

If your bill is much higher

If your final bill is at least $400 more than the estimate, you can start a federal patient-provider dispute resolution process. You generally have 120 calendar days from the date on the bill to do so. Keep a copy of your estimate. The details are on the CMS No Surprises website.

Tax-Advantaged Accounts

Using HSA and FSA Funds

Health savings accounts (HSAs) and flexible spending accounts (FSAs) let you pay for qualified medical expenses with pre-tax money. IRS Publication 502 describes what generally counts. In general terms:

  • Usually eligible: care to diagnose or treat a medical condition, such as physician visits, physical, occupational and speech therapy, and psychotherapy or counseling for a mental health condition.
  • Often depends: treatments your physician prescribes for a specific medical condition, such as hyperbaric oxygen therapy for a cleared indication.
  • Usually not eligible on their own: vitamins or supplements taken for general health. If your physician orders a treatment for a diagnosed condition, your plan may ask for a letter of medical necessity.

Every plan has its own rules. Check with your plan administrator before you pay, and keep your itemized receipts.

Paperwork

What Is a Superbill?

A superbill is a detailed, itemized receipt. It lists the provider's information, the dates of service, standard service codes (CPT codes) and diagnosis codes (ICD-10 codes), and what you paid. People who pay out of pocket sometimes send a superbill to their health plan to ask about out-of-network reimbursement, or keep it for HSA and FSA records.

A superbill does not promise any reimbursement. Whether your plan pays anything back depends on your plan's out-of-network benefits, deductible and rules. Call your plan first and ask how out-of-network claims work. If you'd like a superbill, ask our front desk whether one is available for your visit.

Questions

Self-Pay Pricing: Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Good Faith Estimate?

A written, itemized estimate of expected charges that providers must give uninsured and self-pay patients under the No Surprises Act, before scheduled care or when you ask.

What if my bill is higher than my estimate?

If it is at least $400 higher, you can generally start a federal dispute process within 120 calendar days of the bill date. Talk to us first; billing questions can often be cleared up quickly.

Can I use my HSA or FSA?

Generally yes for care that diagnoses or treats a medical condition, such as therapy and physician visits. Confirm with your plan administrator before you pay.

Why don't you list prices online?

Costs depend on the service and your plan of care. Call us for current pricing; we review costs with you, in writing when you are self-pay, before treatment.
