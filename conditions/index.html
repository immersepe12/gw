Conditions Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy Is Cleared For, and How Physicians Decide

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is cleared by the FDA and recognized by the Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical Society for a specific, published list of conditions, not for everything you may see advertised. Some are outpatient indications a physician can evaluate at our Modesto center. Others are emergencies handled in hospital chambers.

Physician-directed care under Dr. Rajwinder Singh Bahia, MD · Updated September 17, 2026

The Official Lists

The FDA-Cleared and UHMS-Recognized Indications

Three bodies publish lists of conditions for hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT). The FDA clears hyperbaric chambers for 14 conditions. The Undersea and Hyperbaric Medical Society (UHMS) recognizes 15 indications in its 15th edition, which added avascular necrosis. Medicare's National Coverage Determination 20.29 covers 15 conditions when set criteria are met. The lists overlap a great deal, but they are not identical, so the table below shows each one side by side.

The "Typical setting" column describes where each condition is usually managed. It is a general fact about hyperbaric medicine, not a claim about which conditions any one center accepts.

Medicare's list also includes a few items that appear under different names elsewhere: cyanide poisoning, actinomycosis (as an adjunct), and acute peripheral arterial insufficiency. For Medicare's exact wording and criteria, read Medicare-covered HBOT conditions.

What "FDA-cleared" means, and why "approved" is the wrong word

The FDA regulates hyperbaric chambers as medical devices. It clears a chamber for specific uses, one indication at a time. It does not approve "HBOT" as a general treatment for anything. So the accurate phrase is "an FDA-cleared indication for hyperbaric oxygen therapy." The FDA also advises patients to receive HBOT under a doctor's care at an accredited facility, and warns consumers about centers that advertise uses outside the cleared list.

Pressure matters too. The UHMS defines HBOT as breathing near-100% oxygen in a chamber pressurized to at least 1.4 times normal atmospheric pressure (1.4 ATA), and every UHMS-recognized indication calls for 2.0 ATA or more.

Outpatient Indications Our Physician Evaluates

  • Non-healing wounds. Which chronic wounds are cleared categories, and which are not.

  • Compromised skin grafts and flaps. A time-sensitive adjunct to your surgeon's care.
  • Sudden sensorineural hearing loss. Considered alongside steroids from your ear, nose and throat doctor, within guideline time windows.

Emergencies

Emergency Indications: Call 911

Carbon monoxide poisoning, decompression sickness, air or gas embolism, necrotizing soft-tissue infections, gas gangrene, crush injuries, severe anemia, central retinal artery occlusion and acute burns are medical emergencies. If you or someone near you may have one of these, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. For suspected poisoning, California Poison Control is available at 1-800-222-1222.

Emergency hyperbaric treatment is delivered at hospital-based chambers, alongside emergency and critical care teams. Generations Wellness is an outpatient center and does not provide emergency hyperbaric treatment. Our educational pages on these conditions explain what each one is and why hospitals may use HBOT, so that you and your family understand the care you may receive elsewhere.

Honest Limits

What HBOT Is Not Cleared For

The FDA states that HBOT is not cleared for cancer, autism, Lyme disease, Alzheimer's disease, stroke, brain injury, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, depression, migraine, heart disease, asthma, HIV/AIDS, sports injuries or COVID-19. Wellness claims such as anti-aging or faster athletic recovery are not on any cleared or recognized list either.

Some of these topics are being studied, and a few early studies are interesting. Still, none is a cleared indication today, and Generations Wellness does not market HBOT for them. If you want to understand what the studies do and do not show, read what the evidence says about non-cleared uses.

Candidacy

How Our Physician Decides Whether HBOT Is Appropriate

  • The indication. Is your diagnosis on the cleared or recognized list, and is it a condition suited to outpatient care?
  • The criteria. Many indications have conditions attached, such as a wound grade, time on standard care, or a time window after symptoms began.
  • A safety screen. Lungs, ears and sinuses, medications and implanted devices are reviewed. See contraindications and screening.
  • Coordination with your doctors. With your permission, we work with the doctors already treating you so HBOT fits their plan.

You don't need a referral to call us, though many patients come with one. Doctors who want to discuss a patient can refer a patient. On payment: Generations Wellness currently offers HBOT on a self-pay basis and does not bill insurance or Medicare. Coverage elsewhere depends on the indication and criteria, which we explain in which indications insurance may cover.

Important

A physician assessment is required before beginning HBOT at Generations Wellness. HBOT complements, and never replaces, the treatment prescribed by your doctors.

Questions

Cleared Conditions: Frequently Asked Questions

What conditions are FDA-cleared for HBOT?

The FDA has cleared hyperbaric chambers for 14 conditions. They include select diabetic lower-extremity wounds, delayed radiation injury, compromised grafts and flaps, refractory osteomyelitis and sudden sensorineural hearing loss, plus emergencies such as carbon monoxide poisoning and decompression sickness.

Is HBOT FDA approved?

The FDA clears hyperbaric chambers as medical devices for specific indications. It does not approve HBOT in general, and it has not cleared HBOT for many conditions marketed elsewhere.

Do I need a doctor's referral?

Not to call us. A physician assessment at our center is required before any course, and with your permission we coordinate with your treating doctor.

Is my condition managed here or at a hospital?

Emergency indications are handled in hospital chambers, so call 911 for those. Wound, radiation injury, graft and flap, bone infection and sudden hearing loss indications can be evaluated at our Modesto center.

Does Generations Wellness accept insurance for these conditions?

Not currently. We offer HBOT on a self-pay basis while we work toward insurance contracts. Ask us and we'll explain your options honestly, including when coverage elsewhere may make more sense.
